A perfect week, spent on nothing

Klingenberg Beat Everybody, and the Only Race That Moved Was the Ugly One

The biggest score in the league belonged to an eliminated team, and the entire week resolved into a straight swap between Keith Creelman and Shawn Blake for a division bid neither of them has earned.

The Lead

Chris Klingenberg had the best week of his season and it bought him absolutely nothing. Patrick Mahomes handed him 38.6, Zach Ertz added 28.8 behind him, Dak Prescott piled on from a third slot, and when everything was totalled Klingenberg had outscored every other roster in the league. His playoff odds sit at 0%. He is the most dangerous dead man the format has produced this year.

Brian Cross got the single best line of the week out of A.J. Brown: 10 catches on 12 targets for 132 and 2 TD. Brown came into this averaging 13.8, which is the whole point — this was not a star doing star things, it was a fine player having the day of his season on the one card that needed it. It was his best week of the season, and Cross won all three of his games with it.

Derrick Whitman got 37.2 from Jordan Love and lost to the entire field anyway. Nobody else in the league went winless against everyone; Derrick Whitman managed it alone, on a card that could not have beaten a single opponent even played perfectly. Matt Gerardi at least had Bryce Young for 29.1, and he still took the largest beating on the board.

The wire moved 22 bodies this week and 2 trades cleared, none of which changed a result. The best thing anyone bought was Eric Mannila's Bam Knight (17.9), and Mannila left him on the bench, which is either discipline or forgetfulness and he is welcome to pick.

Here is what all of it added up to. Two playoff numbers moved more than a shrug: Keith Creelman shed 13 points and Shawn Blake picked up 13 points. Everybody else is either locked in, locked out, or moved by a point in one direction. The whole slate — the perfect week, the largest margin of the week, a man losing to eleven opponents at once — came out the other side as one swap between two teams with losing records.

The Games

Josh Martel started Rome Odunze and benched Jameson Williams, and it is the one lineup call this week that anyone should be sore about. Williams went for 30.1. Odunze managed 3.0. The verdict is not kind: the wrong man started -- Jameson Williams had been the better player all year, and that single change wins him the game against Klingenberg.

The rest of the bench damage was honest bad luck. Ryan Bousquet started the better player and got nothing from him; Derrick Whitman did the same; Brian Cross and Matt Gerardi both played the man the season told them to play. Six losers had a winning lineup available and did not set it — six of them, most by accident rather than judgement — while four were simply outgunned and could not have won with a flawless card. Only one man in the league actually chose wrong.

The Landscape

Bear Dunn went two-and-nine against the field, lost his division game to nobody because he did not play one, and still runs DOG POUND 4 LIFE at Bear Dunn (26-9). Ryan Bousquet is behind him with a worse division record and no realistic route past it. In ICE UP SON!, Eric Martel (23-12) holds the bid, and the man closest to him on division record is Derrick Whitman, who is riding a 5-28 three-week run and cannot get in by any door.

Brian Cutting sits on top of LEGION OF BOOM at Brian Cutting (22-13), unbeaten in the division and coming off the worst week of his season, which is the luxury of a lead built in September's arithmetic rather than this one. Josh Martel is the second-best team in that division and the hottest team in the league at 28-5. Matt Gerardi is 0-8 in it and has been out for a while.

MAKE IT RAIN is the only race anyone should be watching, and it is not pretty. Shawn Blake leads it at Shawn Blake (14-21), on the strength of a division record his overall record does not deserve; Keith Creelman is right behind at 5-3 with more points scored and worse timing. Chris Klingenberg, who beat every team in the league this week, is 1-7 in that division and eliminated. Three teams, one automatic bid, and the winner will be somebody's first-round bye snack.

Everything else is settled or nearly so. Cutting, Eric Martel, Dunn and Josh Martel all read 100%, with Josh Martel the only one of them who gained ground this week. Bousquet is at 94% and lost a point he will not miss. Mannila is at 6% after going eight-and-three against the field, which is the harshest sentence the simulation handed down all week.

So the whole slate came down to a swap. Creelman gave back 13 points and Blake collected 13 points, and if you had watched nothing but those three MAKE IT RAIN cards you would know everything that changed. Klingenberg's perfect week, Cross's best week of the season, Whitman losing to eleven opponents at once — none of it altered a single seed. The bottom of this league is where the last drama lives.
