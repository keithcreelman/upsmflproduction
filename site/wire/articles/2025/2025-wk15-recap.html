The Lead

Brian Cross got 59.1 out of Trevor Lawrence in a game whose only stake was not finishing last. Lawrence had averaged 19.8 for him all season and had never once cleared 29.9 in a week, and then he turned in 20-of-32 for 330, 5 TD; 5 carries for 51 and 1 TD. It bought Cross a consolation win. The biggest performance of the round belonged to a man who cannot win the thing.

Eric Martel, meanwhile, spent his afternoon beating everybody. He went 11-0 against the field, the only owner who did. Trey McBride caught twelve of thirteen targets and scored twice for 47.0, C.J. Stroud added 32.8, and the whole thing finished +67.5 above Martel's own season average — a career week arriving in the precise round where it was worth the most.

Derrick Whitman went the other direction as far as it is possible to go, losing to all eleven opponents at 0-11 and finishing -27.7 under his own average in an elimination game. Keith Creelman had posted his season wrap-up days earlier and flagged the pick he enjoyed most, which has aged into something close to prophecy. Four lineups around the league included a man who never took the field. Two of those owners won anyway.

The waiver wire moved 28 players and produced almost nothing worth discussing, with one exception: Eddy Pineiro (15.0), bid on by Josh Martel and — crucially — actually started, in a week Martel put up 264.7 behind Jared Goff's 34.7. Brian Cutting grabbed a free agent who matched that output and left him on the bench, which is easier to forgive given Cutting had no game to lose.

The Games

Ryan Bousquet started Joe Burrow in a playoff game and got 0.8 for it. Bo Nix sat on his bench and scored 41.5. This was not variance dressed as a blunder: the wrong man started -- Bo Nix had been the better player all year. Amon-Ra St. Brown gave Bousquet 46.2 and it was spent on nothing.

The four losers split cleanly, and it holds every way you check it. Bousquet and Shawn Blake, the two who had winning lineups sitting on their benches, are the two now out of the title race. Whitman and Matt Gerardi, the two who could have played flawless cards and still lost, are the two sliding toward the floor. The men with a say in their own fate did not exercise it; the men without one never had a chance.

Creelman had no matchup at all this round and made his allegiance clear enough, while Cross spent his week wondering whether the rest of the league could even see his transactions.

The Landscape

Bear Dunn sat the round out and scored 249.4 doing it, which is the fantasy football equivalent of revving the engine in the driveway. DOG POUND 4 LIFE belongs to Bear Dunn (28-9) and he is alive for the title without having had to prove anything this week. Cutting is through on the same terms. Eric Mannila also had a bye, and still managed to bench Bijan Robinson for a man who finished in the red.

Four remain: Eric Martel, who is alive for the title off the best week anyone had; Josh Martel, alive for the title on the strength of 10-1 against the field; plus Dunn and Cutting, rested. LEGION OF BOOM is led by Brian Cutting (22-15) and contains both the best-positioned man in the bracket's lower half and the worst team in the league, which is a division doing a lot of work in both directions.

MAKE IT RAIN went to Shawn Blake (16-21), a division title won at a losing record, and Blake is now out of the title race. Bousquet's exit stings differently: he came in level with Eric Martel (24-13) on record, better than him on all-play, and is out of the title race anyway. A division crown and a losing record get you a seat; nothing gets you a second round if the lineup is wrong.

Down at the bottom, Whitman is sliding toward the cellar and Gerardi is sliding toward the cellar, and neither had a card in hand that would have changed it. Klingenberg, Cross, Creelman and Mannila are all clear of the trapdoor. That fight is now between two men who could not score, which is the fairest thing about the whole week.
