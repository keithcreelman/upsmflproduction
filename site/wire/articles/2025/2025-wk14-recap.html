The Lead

Bear Dunn started Josh Allen and Allen handed him the top line anyone produced -- 22-of-28 for 251, 3 TD; 9 carries for 78 and 1 TD. That came to 45.4 from a quarterback already averaging 27.6 before kickoff, which is how a good week becomes a clean sweep. Dunn went 11-0 against the field.

He did not need the rest, and got it anyway. Tony Pollard chipped in 31.9 and Jaxon Smith-Njigba 29.1, and Dunn left almost nothing behind him -- 6.1 on the bench, total. The best thing anyone pulled off the wire out of 19 adds was also his, and it was in his starting lineup the same week he bought it: Alex Highsmith (19.0).

Derrick Whitman went the other direction as far as the format allows. He lost to every opponent on the board, finishing -63.7 against his own season average on a night Jordan Love was busy throwing scores for him. Keith Creelman was barely better off, 1-10 on the day, and he can't even point at his own bench: he left 2.1 there. Brian Cutting had Jahmyr Gibbs go for 37.0 with three touchdowns in it and lost twice.

The only genuinely careless lineups in the league belonged to Brian Cross, who started Josh Sweat (DE), Dontayvion Wicks (WR) and won both of his games regardless, and Eric Mannila, who started Will McDonald (DE) and lost both by more than any inactive could explain. Nobody else made a mistake. That is not the same as nobody paying: four of this week's losers had a winning lineup somewhere in their roster, and four more could have played a flawless card and still gone home.

The Games

Brian Cutting lost twice on the same slate, both times to men from his own division, and neither loss was his fault in any way a manager can fix.

By the only test that means anything -- did the better player, on his season to date, take the field? -- the number of wrong bench calls in this league came to not one of them. Ryan Bousquet is the headline case and the least deserving of grief: Shedeur Sanders put up 43.2 in a suit while Bo Nix managed 18.2, and the right man started and had a bad day. Cutting sat Zay Flowers for Justin Jefferson. Chris Klingenberg sat Dawson Knox for Zach Ertz. Every one of those is the call you make again next week.

So the punishment landed nowhere near the crime. The men who forgot to check their rosters were Cross, who swept, and Mannila, who was beaten by so much in both games that a full lineup would not have mattered. The men who actually lost winnable games did so by starting the right people on the wrong day.

The Landscape

Bear Dunn tightened the only division lead nobody is arguing about, Bear Dunn (28-9), with Bousquet and Mannila both losing to him on the same slate and Bousquet stuck at 4-6 inside it. Eric Martel keeps ICE UP SON! at Eric Martel (24-13) despite going 2-9 against the field, which is the mercy of playing Whitman and being caught by nobody who can hurt him -- Cross is 17-20, Whitman 14-23. Those two bids are effectively spoken for.

LEGION OF BOOM is the live one. Brian Cutting holds it, Brian Cutting (22-15), and Josh Martel now sits on the identical overall record with the division record the tiebreak -- Cutting 8-2, Martel 6-4. Martel took the head-to-head this week and is riding the best three-week stretch in the league at 28-5. Cutting was unbeaten in the division at breakfast and is not now.

Then there is MAKE IT RAIN, where Shawn Blake (16-21) means an automatic bid is currently the property of a team under water, while Eric Mannila at 18-19 leads nothing at all. Blake earned it inside the division, 8-2, and that is the number that counts. Keith Creelman is somehow still theoretically in it at 5-5 despite losing to ten of eleven opponents on the day, which reflects on the division rather than on the commissioner.

Everything after the four bids runs on all-play, and that is where the records lie to you. Cutting has taken more all-play beatings from nobody -- he is still ahead of Dunn on that column after losing twice, which is the strongest argument that his week was variance. Bousquet's 24-13 and Klingenberg's 13-24 describe two very different seasons than their weekly all-play marks do. And at the bottom, Whitman is 0-33 over three weeks and Gerardi is 10-27 with a division mark of 1-9; one of them has a bad month and the other has a bad year.
