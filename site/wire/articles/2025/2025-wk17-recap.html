The Lead

Brian Cross started Derrick Henry and Henry handed back 36 carries for 216 and 4 TD. That was the biggest score of the week, out of a back whose best game before it was 32.2 and whose average sat at 15.9. It bought Cross a consolation win. That is the entire return.

Eric Mannila picked the same week to detonate. He scored 294.4, went 11-0 against every other roster in the league, and did it in a bracket where the prize is not being last. Bijan Robinson supplied 43.3 of it and Mannila still leaves the year as a man who was never in the title race.

The half of the field that was actually playing for something was quieter about it. Brian Cutting got 45.7 out of Drake Maye, added 28.9 from Chris Olave, and that was the season. Josh Martel came back with 31.6 from Caleb Williams and Christian McCaffrey behind him and was never in front. Eric Martel won his game with Trey McBride's 29.4 and a lineup below his own standard.

Shawn Blake, who was not playing a game, got 43.2 from Brock Purdy. Ryan Bousquet, also idle, put 227.9 on the board and went 9-2 against the field for nothing at all. Matt Gerardi's idle week was a different flavour: 0-11 against everybody, which takes a certain commitment.

The wire moved anyway — 34 of them changed hands, and the best of the lot was Malik Willis (35.6), bid for and started by a man who was beaten by a wide margin regardless. Derrick Whitman's roster mustered 158.3 while not playing, which is its own kind of statement.

The Games

Bear Dunn left a winning lineup on his bench. He is the only man in the round who can say that, and that is the whole week.

Of the four men who lost, three of them could have played a flawless card and still gone home. Exactly one beat himself. And of every bench swing in the round — the ones that look ugly in a screenshot and get quoted back at you for a year — not one of them was actually the wrong call. The men who started busted, and the men on the bench went off, and in every case the starter was the better player going in.

The loudest of them belonged to Shawn Blake, who benched Chris Godwin for 26.8 and started Jaylen Waddle for 0.7. The verdict on it is the right man started and had a bad day, and Blake was not playing a game anyway. There is only one genuinely indefensible lineup decision in the round, and it did not change a result either.

Bousquet set himself a resignation condition before any of this and then failed to clear the bar in the safest possible direction.

The Landscape

Brian Cutting finished the year as Brian Cutting (22-15) and then finished it 1st, which is the tidiest thing anyone did all season. He took the division by a clear margin, took his division rival in the last game that mattered, and never had a week where the whole thing wobbled. Josh Martel is 2nd, out of the same division, with more points scored across the year than the man who beat him. That is a hard second place to sit with.

Shawn Blake won MAKE IT RAIN with a losing record — Shawn Blake (16-21) — and finished 5th. Ryan Bousquet won more games than Blake did by a distance and finished 6th. Anyone still arguing that won-lost record is the standings has now been shown the receipt.

Bear Dunn ran up the gaudiest record in DOG POUND 4 LIFE, Bear Dunn (28-9), and landed 4th — a season of beating his own division and one bracket game where his bench outscored his lineup. Eric Martel won ICE UP SON! and came out 3rd, one place better than Dunn on the back of the ten points he found this week. Eric Mannila, who beat all eleven other rosters on the final weekend, is 7th.

At the other end, Matt Gerardi ends 11th after a year in which his own division took him apart, and Derrick Whitman is 12th with an all-play record that reads like a typo. Whitman scored the fewest points in the league across the season and lost to essentially everybody, essentially always. The toilet bowl question raised in the chat has an answer now, and it is not Bousquet.

The verdict on the year: the champion was the most consistent team in the field rather than the highest-scoring one, the highest-scoring weekend of the finals belonged to a man who could not use it, and the only lineup that lost a game its owner had won was set by the man with the best record in it. Nobody got robbed. A few people got outscored.
