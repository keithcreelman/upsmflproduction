UPS Wire
A week that never crossed a division line

Eric Martel Beat Everybody. Matt Gerardi Beat Nobody.

Every matchup on the board was interdivision, Ryan Bousquet's Bo Nix put up the loudest single number in the league, and the race at the bottom of MAKE IT RAIN did not move an inch.

The Lead

Eric Martel went 11-0 against the field, and it started before most of the league had even set a lineup. Ja'Marr Chase went for 42.9 on Thursday night, and Martel spent the rest of the weekend just watching the number hold up. He finished +96.4 against his own average, his best week of the season, and left almost nothing behind him: 3.5 stranded on a bench that barely mattered.

Ryan Bousquet's week broke later and louder. Bo Nix did not play until the Sunday-afternoon window closed in on itself, and when he was done he had the single best line in the league: 27-of-50 for 279, 2 TD; 5 carries for 48 and 2 TD, good for 46.4 on a card that had been averaging 21.3. Bousquet used it to hang the largest margin of the week on Matt Gerardi, 130.7, the largest of 18, and to post his best week of the season.

Gerardi's weekend never had a chance to turn. He went 0-11 against the field, his worst week of the season, the fewest points in the league, and could not have won with a perfect card — the best lineup on his bench still finished behind every score that beat him. Somewhere in the league chat there is a franchise-relocation joke waiting to be made about LEGION OF BOOM's basement tenant, and if nobody has made it yet, give it until Tuesday.

The wire moved 39 bodies and 2 deals cleared, and the only pickup that actually produced was Derrick Whitman's Will Reichard (19.5), started and cashed the same week he claimed it. Ryan Bousquet is the hottest team in the league over the last three weeks at 25-8; Keith Creelman is the coldest at 11-22, a stretch ugly enough that his own group chat has stopped asking him what happened and started just sending condolences.

The Games

The week opened on a Thursday split screen: Ja'Marr Chase lit the board for Martel while Joe Flacco kept Keith Creelman's card afloat for exactly one night, Joe Flacco (35.2), before the rest of his roster spent the weekend proving Thursday was the peak. The early Sunday window belonged to Patrick Mahomes and to a receiver room in Philadelphia that Brian Cross and Derrick Whitman both leaned on at once. Nothing was settled by kickoff of the late window, when Bo Nix turned in the number that decided the week's biggest blowout and Eric Mannila quietly put together his 2nd-best week of the season behind Justin Herbert. Sunday night belonged to Christian McCaffrey, spent on a Josh Martel card that lost every game it played this week regardless. And the week did not actually finish until Monday's second game, ten o'clock kickoff, when Jaxon Smith-Njigba closed the books on Bear Dunn's worst week of the season — one of the last men in the league to find out how his week had gone was also the closest thing this slate had to a contender. Every result below crossed a division line; nobody on this list beat a rival who shares his own standings group.

Brian Cutting had the highest-scoring team in the league walk into the week and still lost twice. Jahmyr Gibbs (39.8) did what he does, and it was not enough against Bo Nix in the biggest game on the board — 47.8, 9th largest of 18. Cutting was -9.5 his own average in the loss; this was simply a bad week wearing a good record.

Cutting's only win of the week came two games later, against a Bear Dunn roster having his worst week of the season. 48.0 decided it, and Cutting did not need to be good to get it — Dunn's card was simply worse. A win manufactured entirely by the other guy's week, not his own.

Eric Mannila closed out a rough week for Cutting's roster, 12.3, behind Justin Herbert (38.1). Cutting's best player put up the second-best number of the week on the losing side and it did not matter — could not have won with a perfect card. Two losses bookending one gift of a win, and LEGION OF BOOM's top seed heads into next week having proven a lot less than his record suggests.

Ryan Bousquet's other blowout, 104.8, buried Josh Martel under Bo Nix's number a second time. Martel had the best individual score in the game — Christian McCaffrey (41.5) — and still lost by the length of a football field. His bench told the real story: Oronde Gadsden sat for 36.9 behind George Kittle's 0.0, the biggest swing on the board, and the right man started and had a bad day.

Bear Dunn's second win, over the same Josh Martel, came by the tightest margin of his week — 9.0. This is the one that single change wins him the game: put Gadsden in for Kittle and Martel wins it. He didn't, and a bad bench call in one game turned into a loss in a completely different one.

Derrick Whitman's closest win of the week, 5.4, came off DeVonta Smith (38.3) against a Shawn Blake card that never got going. Whitman left 58.3 on his own bench and still had enough. Blake's whole week can be read in one number: -19.1 his own average, in a game he lost by less than a converted first down.

Then Eric Martel found Shawn Blake a second time and buried him by 124.1, the widest margin outside the Bousquet-Gerardi game. Ja'Marr Chase (42.9) did the damage. Blake has now lost to both of the league's two best weeks in the same slate — bad scheduling luck stacked on top of a bad week.

Whitman's other win, over Keith Creelman, was closer — 8.4 — and came off the same DeVonta Smith (38.3) line. Creelman's best number, Joe Flacco (35.2), could not keep pace. His week did not get better from here.

It got worse two games later, when Eric Martel ran his week to 11-0 by putting Creelman away a second time, 127.1. Ja'Marr Chase (42.9) did the heavy lifting again. Creelman is now on the wrong end of the coldest stretch in the league and it is starting to show up in more than one column.

The week's biggest number belonged to the week's biggest blowout: Bousquet over Gerardi, 130.7, the largest of 18. Bo Nix (46.4) did what it did, Gerardi's best player, Michael Pittman (21.8), was not close, and could not have won with a perfect card. There was no version of this one that ends differently.

Chris Klingenberg's best win of the week, over Derrick Whitman, came down to a bench call that actually mattered: 13th largest of 18 in margin, decided in part by Whitman starting George Kittle over a healthier option. Patrick Mahomes (35.4) outscored DeVonta Smith (38.3) by enough to make the difference — 33.5.

Eric Martel's third win, closing out his perfect week, came against that same Klingenberg team, 85.2. Ja'Marr Chase (42.9) was the story again — this is what a card running +96.4 over its own baseline does to a division leader on a good week. Klingenberg still had the highest of any loser this week, which tells you how good his roster actually was; it just ran into the best week in the league.

Bear Dunn closed his own week the way it opened, beating Matt Gerardi by 34.9 behind Jaxon Smith-Njigba (31.5). Two wins, two ugly scores, and a division record that says more about Dunn's season than either of them did.

Eric Mannila's best win of the week, 69.3, came at Josh Martel's expense and off Justin Herbert (38.1) — the same card that carried him to his 2nd-best week of the season. Martel had the single best player in the game, Christian McCaffrey (41.5), and lost anyway. That is three losses in three games for a roster that was never actually the problem.

Brian Cross's first win, over Shawn Blake, went by 34.6 on the back of A.J. Brown (30.9). It was his 2nd-best week of the season, and it came against a Blake team that has now lost its last three games by a combined margin nobody wants to add up out loud.

Cross's second win, over Keith Creelman, was wider — 37.6 — off the same A.J. Brown (30.9) line. Creelman's card, led again by Joe Flacco (35.2), simply did not have the pieces. Three losses on the week for Creelman, and the group chat has noticed.

Eric Mannila's third win, over Matt Gerardi, 95.2, was the least competitive of his week and the most predictable result on the board. Justin Herbert (38.1) was more than enough against a Gride roster that had nothing left to give by the weekend's third meeting.

The week ended the way any good week should: on the closest game of the slate. Chris Klingenberg beat Brian Cross by 4.3, 18th largest of 18, behind Patrick Mahomes (35.4). Cross's own A.J. Brown (30.9) nearly got there. not one of them bad bench calls turned up across the whole league this week — this one just came down to whose good decisions scored more.

The Landscape

Read the four division leaders next to their division records and the tension in this format shows up immediately. Ryan Bousquet (15-4) holds DOG POUND 4 LIFE despite a division record of 1-3 — he is winning the league and losing his own neighborhood. Bear Dunn is the one actually doing damage inside it, 3-1, and still sits behind him on the overall math.

ICE UP SON! is the strangest of the four: Eric Martel (13-6) leads a division where all three teams sit at 2-2 against each other. Nobody inside that group has separated from anybody else — the bid is being decided entirely by what each of them does to the other eight teams outside it, which is exactly backwards from how a division race is supposed to work.

LEGION OF BOOM is the one division that actually looks like a division. Brian Cutting (10-9) is unbeaten inside it at 4-0, and it is propping up a record that would otherwise read as ordinary — his week off the field was bad, his week inside his own group has been perfect all year. Matt Gerardi is the mirror image at the bottom, 0-4, and this week did nothing to change that story.

MAKE IT RAIN is the fourth automatic bid and it belongs, for now, to a losing record. Shawn Blake (8-11) leads it at a division mark that is the best of a genuinely bad group — Chris Klingenberg is right behind him despite his 2nd-best week of the season sitting on his résumé, and Keith Creelman, mired in the coldest three-week stretch anybody in this league is currently riding at 11-22, is closer to the top of this division than his overall record has any business being. Whoever wins MAKE IT RAIN is going to back into a bid nobody else wanted badly enough to take from them.

Zoom out and the all-play table tells a cleaner story than any of the four division races do. Ryan Bousquet is the hottest team in the league over the last three weeks, 25-8, and it is backed by real production — 3rd in the league for points. Brian Cutting still sits on the most points in the league despite the week he just had, which is what a good September buys a bad October. And at the other end, Matt Gerardi's the fewest points in the league is not a slump anymore. This deep into the season, it is a pattern, and the group chat has already stopped being surprised by it.
