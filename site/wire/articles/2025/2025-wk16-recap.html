The Lead

Puka Nacua caught everything thrown near him for Shawn Blake -- 12 catches on 16 targets for 225 and 2 TD -- in a game that leads nowhere at all. He had averaged 24.1 coming into the round and had never cleared 40.6 in a week all season. Blake won anyway. He is not playing for the title and Nacua's afternoon did not change that, which is the cruellest sentence in this entire recap.

Only one owner beat every team in the league this week: Josh Martel. Jared Goff supplied 35.5 of it and Christian McCaffrey another 34.2, and the two of them together turned a playoff game into a demolition. Martel is one of two men left alive for the title.

The other is Brian Cutting, who did it with the whole receiving corps rather than one act. Chris Olave went for 40.2, Ashton Jeanty added 35.4, and Drake Maye chipped in 33.1 on top. Bear Dunn had offered his encouragement before any of it happened.

Down the other end, the only owner to lose to every single team in the field was Derrick Whitman, which is a hard thing to do in a week where the scoring was this generous. The wire produced 23 adds and the best of them, Marlon Humphrey (14.8), spent the round on Eric Martel's bench while Martel lost his season. Some of the losers were simply beaten. The rest of them handed it over.

The Games

Ryan Bousquet started a defensive tackle who never took a snap -- Walter Nolen (DT). Keith Creelman did the same with a defensive end, George Karlaftis (DE). Brian Cross doubled up: his lineup held Bradley Pinion (PN), Geno Stone (S), and neither of them played a down.

Those are exactly the three losers who had a winning lineup available and did not put it on the field. The other three -- Eric Martel, Bear Dunn, Derrick Whitman -- could have played a flawless card and still gone home. It holds all six ways.

Bousquet's bench was the loudest of it. He had Matthew Stafford sitting there, and Stafford outscored the quarterback he actually started by more than the game was decided by; the verdict is that the wrong man started -- Matthew Stafford had been the better player all year. Creelman's swing and Eric Martel's were the other kind entirely -- the man with the better season average started and had a bad day, which is variance and not a decision anybody should be mocked for.

The Landscape

Brian Cutting and Josh Martel are the last two men who can still win this thing, and they share a division. Cutting is its leader of record, Brian Cutting (22-15), on a division mark Martel has not matched; Martel has the heavier scoring behind him. Neither of them has the best won-lost record in the league and neither of them cares.

Bear Dunn owns DOG POUND 4 LIFE at Bear Dunn (28-9) and owns nothing else that matters now. Eric Martel took ICE UP SON! at Eric Martel (24-13) and was knocked out by a team that finished behind him in the win column. Bousquet and Blake are in the same room with the same problem: still in the championship bracket, no longer able to win it. None of it maps to record.

Shawn Blake leads MAKE IT RAIN at Shawn Blake (16-21), a record that should not lead a fantasy division and does, because the four winners get in and the rest of the field is sorted by how often you beat everybody. That is how a man can be 2-9 against the field this week, as Klingenberg was, and still be the one who advanced.

The cellar fight is where the real menace is. Creelman, Cross, Gerardi and Whitman are all sliding toward the cellar, and Whitman has now given the field a week it can build a whole offseason of jokes on. Klingenberg and Mannila are safe from the cellar, having done nothing especially impressive except win when it counted, which is the entire skill. Next round settles a champion and, more expensively, a last place.
