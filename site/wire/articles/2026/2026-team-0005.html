HammerTime Is Stacked Everywhere but Quarterback

Eric Martel sent Jalen Hurts and $20,000 of blind-bid money to The Long Haulers for two 2027 picks in April, turned the savings into Kenneth Walker and David Montgomery, and restructured Ja'Marr Chase and Walker for room. His quarterbacks rank last in the league. The preseason simulation ranks the 2024 champion 4th.

Early Preseason

April was deep everywhere but one spot. Martel carried 20 active players and $219,000 in salary, with 3 top-twelve starters, Ja'Marr Chase, an Elite WR1, Trey McBride, an Elite TE1, and Kenneth Walker at running back, and C.J. Stroud, Javonte Williams, Ladd McConkey, Montgomery and Luther Burden solid around them. The one hole in April was the superflex, where T.J. Hockenson, a tight end, stood in for a second quarterback.

None of the three spring trades was graded by the trade bot at the time; graded now on what was known on each trade's date, they come out A-, D+ and C. In March Martel extended Stroud's rookie deal for one more year at $22,000. In April he sent Jalen Hurts, in the last year of a deal HammerTime had restructured the season before, and $20,000 of blind-bid money to The Long Haulers for their 2027 first and second, then spent his own 2027 first on Walker and his own second on Montgomery, and quietly added a year to Montgomery's deal. Four days after the Walker trade he restructured twice: Chase for 2 years at a TCV of $129,000, back-loaded to $26,000 this year and $103,000 in 2027, and Walker the same way, $15,000 this year and $59,000 next, both totals unchanged. Of his other expiring rookies, Sean Tucker went to Sex Manther at the Expired Rookie Auction and Tyree Wilson was let go. Javonte Williams was already on a $30,000 tag by April, and unlike most tags this summer his was simply a keep: Williams is his second running back and grades as a High-end RB2.

Pre-Auction

On draft night Martel moved up twice, sending 4.01, 4.08, 5.01 and two 2027 picks away for 2.12 and 3.08, trades the bot graded B and B+, and made 4 picks, Skyler Bell and Demond Claiborne first. The only release before the lock was Troy Franklin and his $5,000 salary.

The Auction and Preseason Contracts

HammerTime spent $69,000 at the Free Agent Auction, mostly on depth and defense, and made no restructures during it. Josh Jacobs was the big buy at $30,000, and Aidan Hutchinson, Micah Parsons, Ernest Jones and T.J. Watt came cheaply behind him. Jacobs, a Low-end RB2, is the one Good buy, and the league table ranks HammerTime 9th of 12 for how much the auction improved the offense: the one hole was quarterback, and he did not buy one.

The contracts were long. Jacobs signed for 3 years at a TCV of $90,000, back-loaded: $18,000 this year and $36,000 in 2027, and Hutchinson, Jones and Parsons got multi-year deals too. After the auction he cut Jonnu Smith, whose $6,000 salary carried a $7,500 penalty charged to 2027, and claimed 4 players off waivers, among them Quentin Lake and Jalen Nailor.

HammerTime is stacked everywhere but quarterback. The lineup it built has 3 top-twelve starters, Chase, McBride and Walker, the league's 1st-ranked tight ends and 2nd-ranked receivers, and one hole, the superflex, where a tight end plays instead of a second quarterback. Stroud, a Low-end QB2, is the only startable quarterback on the roster; Michael Penix Jr. is out with a knee, and Geno Smith and Anthony Richardson are not starters, so Hockenson plays the superflex, and by starting-quarterback value HammerTime ranks 12th of 12. Graded on MFL's projections the defense is good, Watt, Jack Campbell and Ernest Jones all starters near the top of their positions, and the punter, Austin McNamara, is a High-end PN1.

The injuries are adding up. Jacobs is suspended for the first two weeks and sits on injured reserve, Parsons is on injured reserve into October, Penix is out for the opener, and Cedric Gray, starting in a flex, is questionable with a concussion. Jalon Walker, on the taxi squad, is out for the season.

History and the Division

Martel took over HammerTime partway through 2022 and has had it for three full seasons since, and he has made the most of them: the 2024 title, a division title in 2025, and the playoffs in every one of them.

He was the first captain in the league's first Owner Divisional Draft, seated first by career all-play, and he made the room's first statement: The Long Haulers 1st overall, which by the bot's own rule of thumb meant he thought they were the weakest team in the draft. With his last pick he took the reigning champion, Pure Greatness, left on the board 8th, after predicting in May that the last team left would be a strong one. In July he called himself and Pure Greatness a coin flip and gave himself the edge. The simulation gives it to Cutting: Pure Greatness 49.5%, HammerTime 30.6%, The Long Haulers 19.9%, with 53.5% playoff odds for HammerTime.

The division weeks are 1, 4, 8, 11 and 14, and week eight is the one to circle: Stroud is on bye, leaving Penix, back from his knee by then, and the replacement-level Geno Smith and Anthony Richardson, and Montgomery and the kicker are off that week too. Week eleven costs him only Ernest Jones and Lake, while The Long Haulers lose four starters, A.J. Brown among them, and Pure Greatness loses Drake Maye. Week fourteen, the last of the regular season, takes Javonte Williams and McBride. Across the five weeks he loses 7 starters to byes, Pure Greatness 6 and The Long Haulers 6.

The next three years: in 2027 HammerTime wins the division on Chase and McBride, still without a second quarterback; in 2028 Martel finally pays up for one; in 2029 HammerTime wins the division again and reaches the final, and loses it.

Week one is a division week. With 4 NFL teams done, Quentin Lake, a waiver pickup, had an interception and HammerTime has 30.5 points in the bank.
