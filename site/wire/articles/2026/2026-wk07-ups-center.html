Stuart

So why does the forecast like Cutting so much?

Rich

Because the forecast does not play the record. It plays each team’s best lineup every week, built from 2026 points per game with everyone healthy. Bear’s is 290.9 a week. Cutting’s is 276.4, second in the league, ahead of Eric Martel (273.4) and Shawn Blake (265.9). Cutting’s roster is better than his 11–8.

Stuart

Is it?

Rich

Both things are true. This season Cutting has left 44.0 points a week on the bench, the most in the league, and in 5 of 8 of his losses the best lineup from his own roster would have won. In Week 7 it was Bucky Irving (26.8) and Deshaun Watson (22.4), 49.2 between them. He swept anyway, with 236.1. In fairness, the starters were the better bets on paper: Chuba Hubbard was projected 16.2 to Irving’s 11.4, Aaron Rodgers 15.1 to Watson’s 14.4. A best lineup is only known afterward.

Kenny

Without Jackson, Aaron Rodgers (19.2) steps in and Cutting’s best lineup drops 5.1, to 271.3, third in the league. He is staying afloat.

Stuart

And the bye?

Rich

Bear is the heavy favorite for the first one, at 97.7%. The second is Eric Martel at 57.1%, Brian Cutting at 33.3% and Shawn Blake at 7.3%. Nobody else is above 2.9%.

Rich

Martel and Cutting are in the same division, Outlast PG Time, with Brian Cross. Martel is 14–5, Cutting is 11–8, three games behind, and the forecast gives Martel 64.0% to win it and Cutting 36.0%. The division winner takes a top seed. That race is the bye.
