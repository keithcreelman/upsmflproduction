Real Deal Creel Bought a Superstar and Shoplifted the Rest

Keith Creelman spent $189,000 at the Free Agent Auction and 7.1% of his money on defense. Real Deal Creel came out 6 of 12. Both of those are on purpose.

The Lineup

Puka Nacua is an Elite WR1 and he is the whole receiving corps. The room ranks 4 in the league on his back, with Christian Watson at High-end WR3 next to him doing a passable impression of a starter. That is this roster in one line: one player nobody can match, and a supporting cast bought at a discount.

Quarterback came out fine, if not the fireworks the price implied. Dak Prescott grades Mid QB1 and Kyler Murray Mid QB2 — a real superflex pair with no passengers, ranking 8. Keith paid $63,000 for Prescott, which is a lot of money for the eighth-best quarterback in a format where everyone needs two of them.

The backfield is the quiet success. Saquon Barkley at Mid RB1, Travis Etienne at Mid RB2, TreVeyon Henderson at High-end RB3, ranking 6 — three genuine starters, in a format where most teams are guessing at the flex by Halloween. Dallas Goedert grades Low-end TE1, the last chair in the room rather than a hole, and the position ranks 9.

Then the defense, and this is where the obvious article is the wrong one. Three of seven defensive slots start players the production model calls replacement-level — Frankie Luvu, Chuck Clark, Gregory Rousseau — with Nick Bolton, Chamarri Conner, Josh Hines-Allen and Jalyx Hunt in the tier behind them. Read that cold and you would say he forgot about defense.

He did not forget. Real Deal Creel has acquired 428 defenders since the league started keeping the log, ranking 1, and the next team on that list has 257. He has led the league in defensive pickups 5 straight seasons. He also spent $14,000 on defenders this year — 7.1% of the largest auction budget in the league. That is not neglect, it is a policy: never pay retail for a linebacker, mine them off the wire instead, all season, forever.

The policy mostly works. Points per started defender over the last three seasons puts him 4 of 12, which is above the median for almost no money and a genuinely good trade. It is also not first, and the teams ahead of him got there by occasionally just buying a good defender. A Week One defense of three replacement-level starters is not a finished product — it is opening inventory for a man who restocks every week until December.

The Bridge

In April he had 14 active players: Goedert, Christian Watson, Henderson, Tetairoa McMillan, Marvin Harrison Jr, Trey Benson, Keon Coleman and Deshaun Watson, against $62,000 of salary. A to-do list, not a team.

The first real move came before any of that, in April, and it was the biggest single piece of business of his offseason even though it looks like a footnote. He sent Kenneth Walker to HammerTime for their first-round pick. Walker was carrying $32,000 a year — the largest annual contract Real Deal Creel has moved — so the deal cleared that off the books and banked a first-rounder in the same stroke. Everything he did in July was paid for by that.

Draft night is where it got loud. He made three trades while the draft was running and used every pick he acquired within the hour. He sent the 1.04 to Gride, who took Jadarian Price with it, and got back Travis Etienne plus the 1.07 — which became KC Concepcion. He moved up in the second for De'Zhaun Stribling and up again in the third for Sonny Styles. Three trades, three picks, all spent the same night.

By the time the Free Agent Auction opened he was at 16 active players and the year had already turned once: McMillan out, Nacua in, Mid WR2 replaced by Elite WR1. That is the biggest single upgrade anyone made all offseason.

Then the auction, and he did not stop until there was nowhere left to put anyone. The roster closed at 35 active men against a maximum of 35 — completely full. Committed cap went from $95,000 to $297,000, leaving $3,000, which is enough for one minimum body and a firm handshake. Studs went from 1 to 4, holes from 7 to 3. Almost nothing he starts today was carried in on an old contract.

The Auctions

Two auctions, two months apart, and they are not the same event dressed differently. The Expired Rookie Auction sells players whose rookie deals ran out — men who did not earn a second contract — and the whole league-wide pool was 14 players. The most expensive one went for $7,000, against $88,000 for the priciest man in the Free Agent Auction. You do not build a team in May; you top up a bench.

Keith's May came to $9,000 for 5 players: Chris Rodriguez, Kayshon Boutte, Michael Mayer, Will McDonald, Kendre Miller. Rodriguez turned into a component of the Nacua trade, which is a decent return for what he paid. The rest are bench or gone, which is roughly what that pool produces for everybody.

The Free Agent Auction in late July is where the team came from: 19 players for $189,000. Prescott alone took $63,000 — 33.3% of the whole budget, and the most contested item he won all summer.

He bought four quarterbacks there. Prescott and Murray start and both grade honestly. Jacoby Brissett, the cheapest of the quarterbacks he kept, grades Replacement-level QB and has not moved off the bench. Mac Jones was released in September. Buying a third and a fourth quarterback after the position was already solved is what happens when the auction is still open and you are bored.

The bottom of the market is where he is genuinely sharp. Cam Little, Tommy Townsend, Nick Bolton, Josh Hines-Allen and Chamarri Conner all came cheap, which is the right way to fill slots ADP refuses to price. Parker Washington is the one bargain with a starting job attached. Same instinct as the defense policy: find the thing nobody is bidding on, take it, move on.

Contracts, Trades, Cuts and the Rookie Class

Keith made 12 contract moves. Cam Little, Trey Benson, Brian Robinson, Charlie Kolar, Jack Strand, Jacob Saylors and Stefon Diggs all signed at the league minimum, and Diggs at that price is the best-value deal in the set by a distance.

Chig Okonkwo is the one to argue about. He grades Replacement-level TE, he sits behind Goedert, and he signed above the minimum — but the deal is front-loaded, so most of its value is paid this season and the back years cost almost nothing. That is a defensible structure for a bet that has not landed yet: expensive now precisely so it stops being expensive later. Still a replacement-level tight end on a multi-year deal, though.

The Puka Nacua paperwork is easy to misread. Shawn Blake extended Nacua in May, before the trade. What Real Deal Creel did was cut Nacua's salary mid-auction — freeing $13,000 of bidding room at exactly the moment it was worth having — then put it straight back afterwards, ending on the salary and total value it started with. A maneuver, not a contract.

There were 6 trades and the three-way is the one people will talk about, because three-way trades essentially do not happen here. It is the only one ever completed through the league's own trade tool, and Keith built it. He sent McMillan, his own first-rounder for next year, Chris Rodriguez and Omar Cooper, and got Nacua. It was not a two-team swap in a costume: C-Town Chivalry separately sent Drake London and $19,000 of blind-bid cash to Blake Bombers to make it balance. Three rosters, cash, and a pick, to move one receiver. He is also not short a first-rounder next year despite spending his own, because the Walker trade in April had already put HammerTime's in the drawer.

The last trade sent George Karlaftis, a startable defender, to the Hawks for a small upgrade in draft slot — which, given how the defense looks in September, reads worse now than it did in August.

The rookie class runs to 5 picks. KC Concepcion came from the best slot of the group and the redraft market has not caught up to him yet. Omar Cooper Jr. was traded before playing a down. De'Zhaun Stribling grades Mid WR3. Sonny Styles and Zion Young are defenders, and neither the redraft board nor the production model can value them — the board does not price the position and the model needs an NFL season that has not happened. Missing information, not a bad grade.

The Verdict

Start where it actually counts. Real Deal Creel sits 2 of 3 in Maddie Mondays, behind Sex Manther and ahead of L.A. Looks, and he plays both of them twice. That division is a bad joke on him specifically: L.A. Looks and Sex Manther are the two best defensive teams in the league by points per started defender, and Keith — the man who has made more defensive pickups than anyone in league history — is third of three at it. He works harder at that position than his two rivals combined and finishes behind both.

League-wide he is 6 of 12, and the offense is not the problem. Nacua is Elite WR1, Prescott Mid QB1, Barkley Mid RB1, and the backfield runs three deep. That is a playoff offense assembled in a single offseason from a roster that in April was mostly a list of names, and the Walker trade paid for most of it.

The weakness is the defense, and the verdict depends on whether you believe the plan. Cheap and 4 of 12 is a good trade. It has also never been first, and he sold a startable defender in August and then opened the season starting three men the wire would replace for free. He will have replaced them by Week Four; he always does. Buy the star, mine the rest. It is a real strategy, and it is one good linebacker short of a division.
