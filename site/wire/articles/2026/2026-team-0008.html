Real Deal Creel Bought a Whole Team and Forgot the Defense

Keith Creelman rebuilt almost every priced slot he owns and spent nearly every dollar doing it. Real Deal Creel came out 6 of 12, and the reason is on the defensive side of the lineup.

The Lineup

The offense is in good order and it starts with Puka Nacua, who grades Elite WR1 and is the reason the receiver room ranks 4 in the league. Christian Watson beside him is a High-end WR3, so the ranking is one great player carrying a modest one, but the great player is genuinely great.

Quarterback is better than the spending suggested it would be. Dak Prescott grades High-end QB1 and Kyler Murray High-end QB2, which in superflex is a real pair rather than a starter and a passenger. The group still ranks 8, which says more about how deep the league is at the position than about the two men Keith bought.

The backfield is the deepest room on the roster: Saquon Barkley at High-end RB1, Travis Etienne at High-end RB2, TreVeyon Henderson at High-end RB3, ranking 6. Three usable backs is a genuine asset in a format where most teams are guessing at the flex by October.

Dallas Goedert grades Low-end TE1 — the bottom of the starter tier, not a hole, and the position ranks 9. Chig Okonkwo behind him grades Replacement-level TE, sits on the bench, and is being paid above the league minimum to do it. Paying real money for a backup tight end who is worse than a free one is the clearest waste in the file.

Then the defense, which is where this team actually loses. Three of the seven defensive slots are filled by players the production model rates replacement-level: Frankie Luvu at linebacker, Chuck Clark at defensive back, Gregory Rousseau in the defensive flex. Replacement-level means the waiver wire supplies that production for nothing. The best defenders here — Nick Bolton at Low-end LB2, Chamarri Conner at Low-end DB2, Josh Hines-Allen and Jalyx Hunt both Low-end DL2 — are second-tier. There is not one defender on this roster that either the market or the record calls good.

The bench holds 30 players' worth of depth and very little of it is useful. Marvin Harrison Jr grades Low-end WR3 on an expensive contract. Jacoby Brissett grades Replacement-level QB and is one of the priciest deals not in the lineup. The composite lands at 6 of 12.

The Bridge

In April the roster held 14 active players. UPS starts eighteen. There was no lineup to assess and no decisions to grade, because a roster that size cannot field a team — the bridge table marks the stage as unable to field one and that is the whole of it.

What was on it were contracts still running: Goedert, Christian Watson, Henderson, Tetairoa McMillan, Marvin Harrison Jr, Trey Benson, Keon Coleman and Deshaun Watson, plus an expired rookie deal on Emanuel Wilson that had run its full three years. Active salary was $62,000.

By the opening of the Free Agent Auction the roster had reached 16 active players, still short of a lineup, but the year had already turned once. McMillan was out and Nacua was in — High-end WR2 replaced by Elite WR1, the largest single upgrade Real Deal Creel made all offseason. Etienne had arrived from Gride with the pick that became KC Concepcion.

Then the auction, and the roster went to 32. Prescott replaced Deshaun Watson, Barkley took the lead backfield slot, Murray filled the superflex, Parker Washington took a flex. Cap committed went from $95,000 to $297,000, leaving $3,000. Studs went from 1 to 4 and holes fell from 7 to 3.

Keep the two questions apart. Almost nothing Keith starts today was carried in on an existing contract — Goedert, Christian Watson and Henderson are the survivors, and none of them is why this team ranks where it does. He built this in one offseason, which is the strongest thing that can be said about it.

The Auctions

Two separate auctions, two months apart, and they read very differently. The Expired Rookie Auction came first in May: 5 lots for $9,000 in total — Chris Rodriguez, Kayshon Boutte, Michael Mayer, Will McDonald and Kendre Miller. Rodriguez was traded inside the three-way before he played a down, Boutte and Mayer both grade replacement-level, and McDonald and Miller were dropped as waiver make-room later in the summer. Nothing Real Deal Creel bought in May is starting in September.

The Free Agent Auction in late July is where the team came from: 19 lots for $189,000. Prescott alone went for $63,000, or 33.3% of the entire free-agent budget, and drew the most bidding of any lot Keith won. He got a High-end QB1 for it, which is the price working.

He bought four quarterbacks there — Prescott, Murray, Brissett and Mac Jones. Two start and both grade well. Brissett grades Replacement-level QB on a contract bigger than most starters', and Mac Jones was released in September. That is real money spent twice on the same problem after it had already been solved.

The cheap end was the smart end: Cam Little, Tommy Townsend, Nick Bolton, Josh Hines-Allen and Chamarri Conner filled specialist and defensive slots at the bottom of the market, which is the right way to fill slots ADP does not price. Parker Washington at a low price is the one bargain with a starting job attached. What nobody did was spend on defense — and the lineup shows it, with three replacement-level defenders starting every week.

Contracts, Trades, Cuts and the Rookie Class

Keith made 12 contract moves. Cam Little, Trey Benson, Brian Robinson, Charlie Kolar, Jack Strand, Jacob Saylors and Stefon Diggs all signed at the league minimum. Parker Washington, Chig Okonkwo, Kayshon Boutte and Michael Mayer did not. Diggs at the minimum is the best-priced deal in the set; Okonkwo above it, for a replacement-level tight end who does not start, is the worst.

The Puka Nacua paperwork is easy to misread. Shawn Blake extended Nacua in May, before the trade. What Real Deal Creel did was cut Nacua's salary mid-auction to free bidding room and put it straight back afterwards, ending on exactly the salary and total value it started with — a temporary manoeuvre to buy room at the auction, not a change to the deal.

There were 6 trades. Kenneth Walker went to HammerTime for their natural first-rounder next year, extended a year immediately before he shipped. The draft-night deal with Gride was the efficient one: a first-round pick out, Travis Etienne and the pick that became KC Concepcion back. Two more with Pure Greatness and Gride were pick shuffling. The last moved George Karlaftis, a startable defender, for a small upgrade in draft slot — and given how the defense finished, selling a startable defender for a pick is a move that reads worse now than it did in August.

The three-way defines the roster. Real Deal Creel sent McMillan, its own natural first-rounder next year, Chris Rodriguez and Omar Cooper, and received Nacua. It was not a two-team swap: C-Town Chivalry also sent Drake London and blind-bid money to Blake Bombers, which is the part that makes the price a real price. Because HammerTime's first-rounder was already banked in April, Keith still holds one for next year.

Removals ran to 20 bodies, of which only 5 were contracts carried into the league year and released. 12 were the make-room leg of a waiver claim and 3 were signed and discarded inside the same offseason. Blake Watson looks like a cut with relief attached and is not — he was on the taxi squad, his salary never counted against the cap, and dropping him freed nothing. Penalties charged to this season come to $3,750, with $2,000 deferred to next season and no bearing on the cap Keith is working with now.

The rookie class runs to 5 picks. KC Concepcion came from the slot with the best historical hit rate of the group and the market has not priced him yet. Omar Cooper Jr. was shipped out in the Nacua deal before playing a down. De'Zhaun Stribling grades Low-end WR3. Sonny Styles and Zion Young are defenders, and neither the redraft board nor the production model can value them — the board does not price the position and the model needs an NFL season that has not happened. That is missing information, not a bad grade.

The Verdict

Real Deal Creel finished 6 of 12, and the offense is not why. Nacua is Elite WR1, Prescott High-end QB1, Barkley High-end RB1, and the backfield runs three deep. That is a playoff offense built inside a single offseason from a roster that could not field a lineup in April.

The bill is the defense. Three starting defensive slots are replacement-level, the best defender on the roster is a Low-end LB2, and Keith sold a startable one in August for a fourth-round pick. Every dollar of a $189,000 free-agent budget went somewhere, and almost none of it went to the side of the ball where he is now starting players the waiver wire would replace for free.

The smaller bill is tight end, and it is self-inflicted: an above-minimum multi-year deal for Chig Okonkwo to sit behind Goedert, when Okonkwo grades below replacement and Goedert is only just inside the starter tier. Buy a star, staff the rest properly, and this is a top-three roster. He did the first half.
