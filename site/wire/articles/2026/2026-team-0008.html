The Lineup

This lineup is one great player and a lot of arithmetic. Puka Nacua grades Elite WR1, which on its own drags the receiver room to 4 in the league, because the man starting beside him is Christian Watson at Low-end WR3. Take Nacua out and the position is bare. That is not a knock on the ranking, it is what the ranking is made of.

The backfield is the one place with real depth rather than one name: Saquon Barkley at Low-end RB1, Travis Etienne and TreVeyon Henderson both Low-end RB2, which is 6 at the position. Three usable backs is a genuine asset in a league where the flex is a weekly problem for most rosters.

Quarterback is where it goes wrong, and it goes wrong expensively. Dak Prescott grades High-end QB2 and Kyler Murray Low-end QB2. In a superflex league, where the position is the whole game, that is 8. Creelman paid $63,000 for Prescott, and Prescott is the better of the two.

Tight end is worse and cheaper to explain. Dallas Goedert grades Replacement-level TE, meaning the waiver wire supplies the same production for nothing, and the position ranks 9. Chig Okonkwo also grades Replacement-level TE, sits on the bench, and is being paid real money to do it. Two tight ends, one bar, neither clears it.

The defense has three starting slots filled by players the model rates as replacement-level: Frankie Luvu at linebacker, Chuck Clark at defensive back and Gregory Rousseau in the defensive flex. Nick Bolton grades Low-end LB2, Chamarri Conner Low-end DB2, and Josh Hines-Allen and Jalyx Hunt both Low-end DL2. There is no defender on this roster the market or the record calls good.

The bench holds the other half of the story. Marvin Harrison Jr grades Low-end WR2 and does not start, which is a better player than either of the flex bodies ahead of him is a fair question the lineup does not answer. Jacoby Brissett grades Replacement-level QB and is the third-most expensive contract on the bench. Everything else back there is replacement-level. The composite lands at 6 of 12.

The Bridge

Start with what was actually there in April: 14 active players. UPS starts eighteen. There was no lineup to assess, no flex to fill and no decisions to grade, because a roster that size cannot field a team. The bridge table marks that stage as unable to field a lineup and that is the whole finding.

What the roster did hold was a set of contracts still running: Goedert, Christian Watson, Henderson, Tetairoa McMillan, Marvin Harrison Jr, Trey Benson, Keon Coleman and Deshaun Watson, plus an expired rookie deal on Emanuel Wilson that had run its full term. Active salary was $62,000.

By the time the Free Agent Auction opened the roster had reached 16 active players, which still could not field a lineup, but the shape of the year had already changed once. McMillan was gone and Nacua was in. That is the single largest upgrade Creelman made all offseason and the stud count did not move an inch to record it, because McMillan graded Low-end WR1 and Nacua grades Elite WR1 — same bucket, different universe. Etienne had arrived from Gride and KC Concepcion had come in with him.

Then the auction hit and the roster went to 32. Prescott replaced Deshaun Watson at quarterback, Barkley took the top backfield slot, Murray filled the superflex, Parker Washington took a flex. Cap committed went from $95,000 to $297,000, leaving $3,000. Studs went to 2 and holes fell to 2.

The two remaining holes are Christian Watson and Parker Washington, both Low-end WR3 or worse in slots that start every week. Keep the two questions apart: almost nothing Creelman starts today was carried in on an existing contract. Goedert and Christian Watson are the survivors and neither is why this team ranks where it does. He built this in one offseason.

The Auctions

Two separate auctions, months apart, and they should be read separately. The Expired Rookie Auction came first, in May: 5 lots for $9,000 total. Chris Rodriguez, Kayshon Boutte, Michael Mayer, Will McDonald and Kendre Miller. Rodriguez was traded away inside the three-way before he played a down, Boutte and Mayer both grade replacement-level, and McDonald and Miller were both dropped as waiver make-room later in the summer. Nothing bought in May is starting in September.

The Free Agent Auction in late July is where the team came from and where the money went: 19 lots for $189,000. Prescott alone was $63,000, or 33.3% of the entire free-agent budget, and he attracted the most bidding of any lot Creelman won.

He bought four quarterbacks at that auction: Prescott, Murray, Brissett and Mac Jones. Two of them start and grade High-end QB2 and Low-end QB2. Brissett grades Replacement-level QB and sits. Mac Jones was released in September. That is the position he spent most heavily on and it is still 8. Spending the largest share of an auction on the position you finish worst at, in the league format that punishes exactly that, is the defining error of this offseason.

The cheap end of the same auction was better. Cam Little, Tommy Townsend, Nick Bolton, Josh Hines-Allen and Chamarri Conner filled the specialist and defensive slots at low prices, which is the correct way to fill slots the market does not price. Parker Washington was the only bargain with a starting job attached, and the market rates that job a hole anyway.

Contracts, Trades, Cuts and the Rookie Class

Creelman made 12 contract moves. Cam Little, Trey Benson, Brian Robinson, Charlie Kolar, Jack Strand, Jacob Saylors and Stefon Diggs all signed at the league minimum. Parker Washington, Chig Okonkwo, Kayshon Boutte and Michael Mayer did not. Diggs at the minimum is the best-priced deal in the set; Okonkwo above it, for a replacement-level tight end who does not start, is the worst.

The Puka Nacua paperwork needs stating precisely, because it is easy to read as something it is not. Nacua's extension was done by Blake Bombers before the trade — it is not Creelman's contract work. What Creelman did was cut Nacua's salary mid-auction to free bidding room and then put it straight back afterwards, ending on exactly the salary and total value it started with. That is a temporary cap manoeuvre to buy room at the auction, not a restructure of the deal and not a contract change at all.

There were 6 trades. Kenneth Walker went to HammerTime for their natural first-rounder next year, with Walker extended immediately before he shipped. The Gride deal on draft night was the efficient one: a first-round pick out, Travis Etienne and the pick that became KC Concepcion back. Two more with Pure Greatness and Gride were pick shuffling. The last one moved George Karlaftis, a startable defender, for a small upgrade in draft slot.

The three-way defines the roster. Creelman sent McMillan, his own natural first-rounder next year, Chris Rodriguez and Omar Cooper, and received Nacua. It was not a two-team swap: C-Town Chivalry also sent Drake London and blind-bid money to Blake Bombers, which is the part that makes the price a real price. Because he had already banked HammerTime's first-rounder in April, he still holds one for next year.

Removals ran to 20 bodies, of which only 5 were contracts carried into the league year and released. 12 were the make-room leg of a waiver claim and 3 were signed and discarded inside the same offseason. Blake Watson looks like a cut with cap relief attached and is not: he was on the taxi squad, his salary was never charged to the cap, and dropping him freed nothing. Penalties charged to this season come to $3,750, with a further $2,000 deferred to next season and no bearing on the cap he is working with now.

The rookie class runs to 5 picks. KC Concepcion grades High-end WR3 already and came from the slot with the best historical hit rate of the group. Omar Cooper Jr. was shipped out in the Nacua deal before playing a down. De'Zhaun Stribling grades Low-end WR3. Sonny Styles and Zion Young are defenders, and neither the redraft board nor the production model can value them at all — the board does not price the position and the model needs an NFL season that has not happened. That is an absence of information, not a bad grade.

The Verdict

No, this is not the strongest team in the league. It finished 6 of 12 and the middle of the roster argues for nothing better. But the offseason was a good one, and both things are true at once: he began with a roster that could not field a lineup and ended with a plausible playoff team.

The advantage is Puka Nacua and it is not close. He is why the receiver room grades 4, he cost four assets and a future first, and he is the only player here who wins a week by himself. Barkley behind him makes the backfield 6, the one other place this roster is comfortably above the middle.

The bill is quarterback and tight end. Creelman spent more at the Free Agent Auction on quarterbacks than on anything else and finished 8 at the position in a superflex league. He then paid above the minimum for a second tight end who grades replacement-level, to sit behind a first tight end who also grades replacement-level, and the position ranks 9. He bought a star and then furnished the rest of the house out of the clearance bin.
