At the desk: Stuart & Rich

Stuart and Rich at the desk: Week 2 brought three interdivisional matchups per team. Blake Bombers led the league with 303.9, Brian Cutting's Pure Greatness went 0–3, and the Giants confirmed Jaxson Dart is out for the rest of the regular season with a knee injury.

1 The open
Stuart

Welcome to UPS Center. Week two was an interdivisional week, with three games per team, and five teams finished interdivisional play unbeaten: Shawn Blake, Derrick Whitman and Matt Gerardi in one slate; Bear Dunn and Chris Klingenberg in the other. One of them stood above everyone: Shawn Blake, whose 303.9 points led the entire league — the 7th-highest regular-season weekly score in UPS history (2010-2026) -- the higher ones were 325.5 in 2021, 320.6 in 2025, 312.8 in 2026, 311.9 in 2022, 310.7 in 2022 and 306.8 in 2023. Rich?

Rich

Thanks, Stu. And it wasn’t just the offense. Josh Allen powered Blake Bombers with 50.7 UPS points — the highest individual score in the league this week. He completed 20 of 31 passes for 248 yards and three touchdowns, then added 69 yards and two more scores on 14 carries. Blake’s IDPs delivered a league-high 85.0 points, led by Devin Lloyd’s 32.0 on 9 tackles, 1 sack, 2 INT, 1 TD.

Stuart

On the other side of that same interdivisional slate, the preseason No. 2, Brian Cutting, did not win once — 0-3. We will get to why in the divisions.

Stuart

Now flip to the other slate, Rich, because Eric Mannila had the roughest week two in the league — 0-3, the fewest points anyone scored. But it was not a uniformly bad week for him: CeeDee Lamb scored 39.7 on a projection of 14.0, the single biggest overperformance in the league by any starter, offense or defense.

Rich

What actually went wrong was an injury, not bad quarterback play. Jaxson Dart started at quarterback over Kirk Cousins — the numbers at kickoff backed that call, Cousins projected 11.2 to Dart's 22.5 — and then Dart left the Monday-night game against the Rams on the Giants’ seventh snap of the game: 5 passes, 3 complete for 20 yards, nothing else. The 1.2 points that followed are an injury-shortened night, not a bad process. Malik Nabers also finished with 1.1 points on a projection of 14.9, but that result needs context. He briefly left with a right-shoulder injury, returned and finished with 43 snaps. Between the injury and Dart’s early exit, this is not a clean performance grade.

Two different measures share this page. Projected → scored compares MFL's own pregame projection, captured before that player's own kickoff, to what he actually scored — that is what "bust" and "bargain" mean, for offense and defense both. Expected points → scored prices what actually happened on the field — every target, carry and throw, how far downfield and where — against what that usage normally scores in UPS play; offense only, since there is no expected-points model for defenders. Started players only in both. Injury-truncated or materially injury-affected performances are excluded from the ranked bust list and shown separately as not graded. The ranked offense-bust list runs three deep this week rather than five for that reason: two names came off the ranked list for injury, and the pregame-projection snapshot needed to identify replacements is no longer available to verify.

5 League landscape — forecast and preview grade
Stuart

Four divisions, four leaders, Rich: Eric Martel (2-3) in Outlast PG Time, Bear Dunn (4-1) in Dog Pound 4 Life, Keith Creelman (3-2) in Maddie Mondays, and Derrick Whitman (5-0) in Legion of Boom — the only unbeaten record in the league.

Rich

Whitman came in at No. 7 in the preseason, Stu. Two weeks in, he owns the league's only unbeaten record, and the model's own preview called it: Derrick Whitman was a 62% favorite over Brian Cross and won 215.3-178.0.

Stuart

Speaking of the model, Rich, it has some explaining to do. The favorites went 6–12 despite being projected for about 10.8 wins. That forecast performed worse than simply treating every matchup as a coin flip.

Rich

Look at WHY before you pile on, though. Eric Martel was a 61% favorite over Shawn Blake and lost 303.9-208.7. Ladd McConkey entered the game managing a rib injury and played 31 snaps — the available evidence does not establish how much the injury affected his final result, and it cost the model about 7 points of that math, but that is a rounding error next to what actually happened: Blake's 303.9 explosion, 95.2 points clear of Martel, is the real reason that call missed — not one late injury tag.

Stuart

Fair. And before anybody calls the whole board broken, Rich: Bear Dunn is still the preseason No. 1 and still looks it, at 90.1% playoff odds — he is not the story that flipped. Cutting is the harder case: 1-4, and still 82% to make the playoffs, because his all-play mark is 14-8 and the model's own signal on him still reads 'Moderate upgrade' — it likes his roster and reads the record as brutal matchups, not a bad team. Whitman is the mirror image: five straight wins, and the signal still reads 'Prior confirmed.' The profile behind those wins — a defense tied for the league's worst preseason projection — has not actually changed the model's mind.

Rich

Which is worth saying plainly, Stu: the other slate, Dog Pound against Maddie Mondays, mostly went to script. The preseason No. 1 swept it. The real upset there was the No. 10, Chris Klingenberg, matching him game for game.

Stuart

How this was built — 5 notes
Sources. MFL scores, rosters, schedules and injury tags; a live season simulation run through completed Week 2 results; the league’s archived chat history (quotes are verbatim, with links).
Projections. Bust & bargain use MFL’s last projection captured before each player’s own kickoff.
Expected points. Each offensive starter’s usage priced against what that kind of usage normally scores in UPS scoring. Offense only.
Preview grade. The Week 2 preview (published before kickoff) graded against what actually happened -- favorites’ record, model confidence, and each featured game’s call.
What is not here. No Week 2 stat-correction recap, because the official corrections for this week have not been checked yet. No Week 3 preview, because the projection snapshot it would need was not available. No transactions segment, either — no trades, waiver claims or free-agent pickups were recorded in the league this week.
