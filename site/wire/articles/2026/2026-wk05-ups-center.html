UPS CENTER

At the desk: Stuart, Rich & Boomer · Kenny Mayne with receipts from the league chat

Bear Dunn is pulling away, three 3–10 teams are standing in historically dangerous territory, and the contenders in between are finding out which players their seasons depend on.

1 The Big Story — where the season stands
Stuart

Five weeks down, and this league has started drawing lines. Bear Dunn went 3–8 against the field in Week 1. Since then he is 42–2. He must be the bus driver, because he is taking this league to school. Three teams are already 3–10, standing in a hole no team has climbed out of since this league went to thirteen games by Week 5. And the teams in between are finding out exactly which players their seasons depend on.

Rich

That last part matters most, because it never shows up in the standings. The contenders behind Bear are strong in different ways and exposed in different ways. Shawn Blake leans harder on two stars than anyone in the league, along with Eric Mannila. Eric Martel led the league in scoring over the first two weeks and ranks 6th over the last three. Brian Cutting keeps losing games his own bench would have won. Bear’s production is spread more evenly, which is one reason his lead looks so sturdy.

Stuart

And then there is Ryan Bousquet, the team on the way up. He was last in scoring over the first two weeks and 7th over the last three, and he has won four of his last five games. But more than a third of that climb belongs to Jaylen Warren, and the back Warren replaced, Rico Dowdle, was practicing again last week.

Rich

So the question is not who won Week 5. It is who is built to keep winning. Four teams are at 90% or better to make the playoffs and three are at 5.1% or lower, and Chris Klingenberg plays three of the six Week 6 games most likely to reshape the field.

Stuart

This is the week the season turned into a playoff race.

2 Playoff Picture — updated after Week 5
Stuart

Bear Dunn is about as close to a lock as this league gets in October: 99.5% to make the playoffs and 83.2% to take one of the two first-round byes. The fight is over who joins him.

Rich

The chat noticed before the computer did. Kenny?

Kenny

Thank you, Rich. Monday, 8:21 in the morning, Shawn Blake made an announcement: “First week was a fluke. Bear has been unstoppable since then”. The forecast agrees: Bear makes the playoffs in 99.5% of the simulated seasons. Shawn got there in one sentence. The computer needed 10,000 seasons.

Rich

Three divisions have clear favorites: Bear wins his in 95.7% of the simulated seasons, Shawn Blake wins his in 84.5%, and Keith Creelman leads Ryan Bousquet 82.2% to 16.2%. The real race is Outlast PG Time, a coin flip: Eric Martel 51.5%, Brian Cutting 48.3%, even though Martel is 9–4 and Cutting 6–7.

Stuart

Hold on. A 6–7 team is a coin flip for the division and has the second-best title odds in the league?

Rich

Because the forecast plays each team’s best lineup every week, and Cutting’s best lineup has averaged 284.2, second in the league. His record reflects the lineups he actually started. The forecast is betting that gap closes.

Stuart

The biggest mover since our Week 3 odds is Shawn Blake, from 72.6% to 93.8%. He went 4–1 over the last two weeks and traded for Derrick Henry on Oct 6, and Henry gave him 22.6 in his first game. The biggest drop is Derrick Whitman’s, from 54.4% to 39.8%, after a 2–3 stretch and Jayden Reed’s move to injured reserve.

Rich

And the middle gets sorted in Week 6, but not only by who wins. Chris Klingenberg is at 54.0% and plays Bousquet, Creelman and Josh Martel. Almost all of his playoff seasons come through a wild card, and wild cards go by All-Play, so how he scores against the whole league matters as much as his three results. In the simulated seasons where he goes 2–1 with a top-three score, he makes the playoffs 74.8% of the time. Where he goes 3–0 with a middle-six score, 57.6%.

Stuart

Wait. Losing a game can beat a sweep?

Rich

For a wild-card team, a loss with a big score can beat a sweep with an ordinary one. Ryan Bousquet is the other case. Most of his playoff seasons, 82%, come from winning the division, which goes by record, so his wins count. A 3–0 week with a middle-six score is worth 33.3%, about the same as 2–1 with a top-three score at 35.4%.

How the forecast works. Weeks 1–5 are fixed to the real results; the other 24 games of each schedule are simulated from current rosters and weekly projections (RotoWire for offensive points, MFL as the backup and for defenders, kickers and punters, MFL for who plays). Each team keeps 70% of its projected edge over an average team, calibrated to UPS 2021–2025. Division titles follow MFL’s order: record, division record, head-to-head, points, All-Play. It does not see trades or waiver moves after the cutoff and treats Questionable players as available. Single-game win chances are rough; they have not been tested game by game.
3 Heat Check — full season and the last three weeks
Stuart

Bear’s last three weeks are the best stretch anyone has put together this season: 32–1 against the field and the league’s top scoring average over Weeks 3 to 5, 274.9 a week.

Rich

And the team Bear just beat by 89.3 is the one whose record is hiding a slide. Eric Martel led the league at 260.8 a week over the first two weeks. Over the last three he is 6th, at 221.9. He is still 9–4 because of what he banked early.

Stuart

And Monday night showed how thin that cushion has become. Martel’s HammerTime went into the night 53.3 points ahead of Chris Klingenberg’s Hawks, with nobody left to play. Klingenberg still had three: Matthew Stafford, Kyren Williams and Dalton Kincaid.

Kenny

With the Rams and Bills tied in overtime at 11:31, Eric filed a request: “🙏 for a pick or fumble 6”. A reasonable ask. He needed one bad throw.

Stuart

Stafford never threw it. As cool as the other side of the pillow: 40 of 54, 383 yards, 3 touchdowns, no turnovers, 39.3 points. Kyren Williams added 19.4, including the touchdown that forced overtime, and the Hawks won by 9.6, the closest game of the week.

Kenny

Eric, the prayer was received. You may need the premium package next week.

Stuart

And that slide is why Martel’s division is a coin flip with Brian Cutting, the reverse case: a team scoring like a contender and losing like an also-ran. Cutting has left 54.0 points a week on his bench, the most in the league, and 5 of his 7 losses came in weeks when his best possible lineup would have won.

Rich

That is the gap the forecast is betting closes: good roster, bad Sundays. Luck has not helped either. Cutting has 1.2 fewer wins than his weekly all-play rates would predict, and only Chris Klingenberg, at 1.3, has been unluckier.

Stuart

Two teams are climbing. Ryan Bousquet went from 12th in scoring over the first two weeks to 7th over the last three, and Eric Mannila from 11th to 5th.

Rich

Mannila’s climb comes with the biggest swings in the league: 5, 0, 6, 11 and 1 all-play wins, week by week. Those swings have an explanation, and it is two players. Going the other way, Matt Gerardi has fallen from 7th to 12th and won 3 of 33 all-play games over the last three weeks.

Kenny

Week 5 is a fair summary. Sunday afternoon, Shawn Blake explained why Matt was starting Malik Willis: “Forced to start this week. Bryce and Mahomes on bye”. Willis gave Matt’s Gride 21.0, which was perfectly respectable. Gride finished with 161.1, the lowest score of the week, and lost all three. The emergency quarterback was not the emergency.

Rich

The emergency was the rest of the lineup. Amon-Ra St. Brown and Omarion Hampton finished a combined 17.6 points under their projections, and Gride is now at 5.1% to make the playoffs.

Stuart

5 Who Is Winning Weeks? — who the contenders depend on
Stuart

Back to those two stars. No contender gets more of its advantage from two players than Shawn Blake: Josh Allen and Brock Purdy create 32.2% of his entire edge over replacement. Eric Mannila is almost identical: CeeDee Lamb and Bijan Robinson supply 31.9%.

Rich

Edge over replacement is what a player scores beyond what an ordinary starter at his position gave that week. By that measure Allen is worth 24.2 points a week to Blake and Purdy 22.0, and Blake has no third quarterback on his roster. So far one has covered for the other: Allen’s 17.1 in Week 3 came with Purdy’s 43.3, and Blake still won two of three.

Stuart

Mannila has had no such cover. In Week 4, Lamb and Robinson combined for 77.2 and Mannila beat all eleven teams. In Week 5 they combined for 18.7, and he beat one team and lost all three of his games. His best bench receiver averages 10.9 when he plays; his best bench back, 10.2.

Rich

Bear is built differently. His top two supply 25.1% of his edge, well below Blake and Mannila, and when Jaxon Smith-Njigba had his worst game, 13.2 in Week 4, Bear still beat ten of eleven teams. One bad week from one star does not sink him.

Stuart

And which players actually flipped results? Christian Watson gave Keith Creelman more than a replacement-level receiver would have in four matchups, and each time the extra production was bigger than Keith’s winning margin. Put an ordinary receiver in his spot and those four wins become losses. Tyler Shough did the same four times for Derrick Whitman.

Rich

So four of Keith’s nine wins, and four of Whitman’s seven, rest on one player each. For Keith, at 84.2% to make the playoffs, that is a cushion he has earned. For Whitman, at 39.8%, it is the margin he is living on.

6 Player Stories — what changed and what happens next
Stuart

Four stories are shaping this race, and the first one is a toe in Pittsburgh.

1. Ryan Bousquet’s climb, and the Steelers’ backfield

Stuart

Ryan Bousquet averaged 172.9 points over the first two weeks and 217.3 over the last three. He was 2–6 after Week 3; he is 4–1 since.

Rich

Jaylen Warren is a big part of why. In the two games he shared the backfield with Rico Dowdle, Warren averaged 9.9 points. In the three since Dowdle hurt his toe, 26.2. That 16.3-point jump is more than a third of Ryan’s 44.4-point climb. And it has decided games: put a replacement-level back in Warren’s spot and Ryan is 4–9 instead of 6–7, with 12 all-play wins instead of 20.

Stuart

Which makes Dowdle’s toe Ryan’s problem. Ryan is at 19.8% to make the playoffs, and he plays Bear, Klingenberg and Mannila in Week 6. His way in is the division, which goes by record, so every win counts: sweep all three and he is at 40.2%; lose all three and he is at 12.8%. Dowdle was ruled out for Weeks 3 and 4, practiced on a limited basis before Week 5, was listed Questionable and still did not play.

Rich

Here is what he would take back. When both backs were healthy, Dowdle had 15 of the 37 early-down carries and 7 of the 17 running-back targets. Without him the job became Warren’s: 43 of 47 early-down carries. But Dowdle has not been a goal-line back. No Steelers running back has had a carry inside the 5 this season, and Dowdle’s red-zone work was one carry. If he returns, the threat to Warren is volume on early downs and in the passing game, not touchdowns at the goal line. And Boomer’s two long touchdowns explain most of Week 5’s 39.6; Warren had no touches inside the 20.

Stuart

Shawn Blake hears the same news differently. Dowdle is on Blake’s roster, but only as depth: 3.7 and 6.6 points from the bench in the two games he played, and Blake has never started him. For Blake, a healthy Dowdle is insurance. For Ryan, he could take back part of the job that has driven the climb.

Rich

So watch Pittsburgh at Tampa Bay on Sunday at 1 PM, after Dowdle’s practice reports Wednesday to Friday. The first drive comes first: Warren has taken the first running-back carry in four of five games. Then his snap share, his routes on passing downs, his carries and targets, and who gets the ball near the goal line. Our forecast already counts Dowdle as available.

2. Brian Cutting: the best player in the league, and the lineup around him

Stuart

Brian Cutting has the most valuable player in the league by edge over replacement. Jahmyr Gibbs has 161.7 points through Detroit’s first five games: 105 carries, 504 yards, 7 rushing touchdowns and 27 catches.

Rich

History says that start is rare, and that it cools. Score every running back since 1999 by this year’s UPS rules and only Priest Holmes in 2002 (176.9) and Christian McCaffrey in 2019 (166.0) had better first five games. The 25 best starts before this year kept about 80% of their pace over the rest of the season. For Gibbs, that would still be 25.9 points a game.

Stuart

So how is the team with that player 6–7? The lineups. In Week 5, alongside Drake Maye, Cutting started Deshaun Watson, who scored 10.5. Lamar Jackson, whom Cutting traded for on Sep 29, was inactive for Baltimore, and the backup who came over in the same trade, Tyler Huntley, played every snap and scored 29.8 on Cutting’s bench. So did Aaron Rodgers. Seven quarterbacks have been on Cutting’s roster in five weeks.

Rich

And Week 6 tests the rest of the roster. Cutting enters it without Gibbs, who has supplied 14.0% of Cutting’s starting points and 18.5% of the team’s edge over replacement; Detroit is on bye. He faces Derrick Whitman, whose playoff probability is 39.8% today. In the simulated seasons where Whitman loses that game, it falls to 32.1%; where he wins, it rises to 51.3%. Cutting’s own odds barely move: 97.6% with a win, 91.6% with a loss.

Stuart

And the Cutting game is only a third of Whitman’s week.

Rich

Right, and for him the size of the score matters as much as the results. Whitman also plays Eric Martel and Brian Cross, and his likeliest way in is a wild card, which goes by All-Play. Go 2–1 with a top-three score and he is at 54.1%. Go 3–0 with a middle-six score and he is at 45.3%.

3. Bear Dunn’s two pass catchers

Stuart

Bear’s run has two engines, and both catch passes. Jaxon Smith-Njigba has 156.5 points in Seattle’s five games: 37 catches on 50 targets, 583 yards and 7 touchdowns.

Rich

Rescore every receiver since 1999 from box scores under this year’s rules, which puts Smith-Njigba’s five games at 154.5, and only Wes Welker in 2011 started faster, with 170.7. The 25 best starts before this year kept about 74% of their pace. For Smith-Njigba that would be about 23.2 points a game.

Stuart

Then there is Brock Bowers, back from a knee injury in Week 3. He scored 36.0, 24.6 and 30.0 on 13, 11 and 10 targets. Bear did not start him right away. In Week 3 he started Sam LaPorta at tight end, who scored 9.1, while Bowers’ 36.0 sat on the bench. Bear won all three games anyway, and Bowers has started every week since.

Rich

Those three games are the best three-game run by any tight end this season, 16.1 points better than Trey McBride’s best. Go back to 1999, take every tight end season’s best three straight games under this year’s UPS rules, and only 28 of 2,685 seasons beat it.

Stuart

And a quiet Thursday night from Smith-Njigba at Denver would not break Bear; his top two carry a smaller share of his edge than Blake’s or Mannila’s. Bear is already all but in. What he is playing for now is a bye and the title.

4. Twenty-four targets

Stuart

Boomer showed you Monday night: Puka Nacua, 24 targets, 17 catches, 138 yards and a touchdown, 41.7 points for Keith Creelman. Boo-yah. The Rams lost to Buffalo, 37–34 in overtime. Keith did not lose at all.

Rich

That almost never happens. In every season since 1999 with tracked targets, which is all but 2003 to 2008, only Brandon Marshall in 2009 saw more in a regular-season game: 28. This season has three 20-target games, and all three belong to UPS rosters: Nacua’s, plus CeeDee Lamb’s 21 in Week 4 and Malik Nabers’ 20 in Week 5, both for Eric Mannila.

Stuart

So does Keith get that again on Sunday?

Rich

Not that much. In the next game after a 20-target game, players averaged 9.3 targets (39 cases). Lamb followed his 21 with 5 targets and 2.9 points. Nacua missed Weeks 2 and 3 with a hip injury and has scored 30.9 and 41.7 since, so the role is real even if the volume falls back. And Keith needs it: he plays Bear, Klingenberg and Mannila, and his lead in the division rests on his record. Sweep them and he is at 92.9%; lose all three and he is at 74.5%.

8 The Survival Line — what a Week 5 position has meant, and what kind of Week 6 changes it
Stuart

Now the hard part. Matt Gerardi, Brian Cross and Josh Martel are all 3–10, with playoff probabilities of 5.1%, 2.5% and 2.0%.

Rich

And history agrees with the forecast. Since the league moved to thirteen decisions through Week 5 in 2021, 8 teams have reached this point with three wins or fewer. None made the playoffs.

Kenny

Josh Martel saw this coming. Monday, 8:24 in the morning: “Not sure I have an all play win since week 1”. Josh, you have five. One in Week 2, one in Week 3, one in Week 4 and two this week. I checked, because despair is not an official scoring category.

Stuart

Is there any way out?

Rich

Not by record. By All-Play, a few have found one: since 2017, only 4 of the 24 teams ranked 10th through 12th in All-Play after Week 5 went on to make the playoffs. All-Play is the better guide this early; among comparable teams from 2020 to 2025, the team with the better All-Play standing after Week 5 became the playoff team 88% of the time, compared with 86% for more points and 85% for the better overall record.

Stuart

And one of those four is the best story in this league’s history book. Bear Dunn, 2017: 11–44 in All-Play after Week 5, the exact record Josh Martel has now. He won his division, entered the playoffs as the 6th seed and finished 3rd.

Rich

Josh would need that and more, and more than one good result. He plays three games in Week 6: Bear, Klingenberg and Mannila. Bear’s way out was the division, and it is Josh’s best one too: in 80% of the simulated seasons where Josh makes the playoffs, he wins the division, and division titles go by record. So the wins count. Sweep all three and he is at 6.7%; lose all three and he is at 0.8%.

Stuart

And the score?

Rich

Counts too. A sweep with a top-three score puts him at 7.9%. A sweep with a middle-six score, 3.7%. A big week lifts his All-Play, and in the simulation it is also a sign of a better team, so the weeks after it go better too. A lucky 3–0 keeps the standings alive. A big score is the kind of week that changes the story.

How this was built — 5 notes
Sources. MFL scores, standings, transactions and injury status; the league’s D1 history (results through Week 5 of each past season); the league’s playoff brackets; nflverse play-by-play, snap counts, injury reports and weekly stats 1999–2026; RotoWire projections via Sleeper and MFL projections for the forecast; the archived league Discord.
History scoring. Player comparisons across seasons use 2026 UPS rules applied to every season’s box scores. That reproduces MFL’s actual 2026 scores exactly in 81% of 952 player-games and within a point in 91%; box scores do not carry the 50-yard touchdown bonus or return yards. Target counts are missing from 2003–2008, so target comparisons skip those seasons.
Routes. Route participation for 2026 is not available in our sources yet, so role changes are measured by snaps, carries and targets.
Forecast. Division titles follow MFL’s tiebreak order (record, division record, head-to-head, points, All-Play); every simulated season crowns exactly one winner per division. Division and title odds are rounded so each group totals exactly 100.0%.
