At the desk: Stuart, Rich & Boomer · Kenny Mayne in the Coffee Shop

A third of the season is gone and the league is splitting in three: Bear Dunn and three 90% teams at the top, three 3–10 teams on life support, and a crowded middle. Current playoff odds, who is hot, the players behind it, and what history says a Week 5 position is worth.

1 The Big Story — a third of the season is gone
Stuart

Thirteen games into a 37-game season, Rich, the league has split into three groups. At the top, Bear Dunn. He lost eight of eleven all-play games in Week 1. Since then he is 42–2 against the field, and our forecast gives him a 99.5% chance at the playoffs and 83.1% at a first-round bye.

Rich

Three more teams sit at 90% or better: Brian Cutting 95.3%, Shawn Blake 93.8%, Eric Martel 90.0%. Cutting is the strange one. He is 6–7, and he has the second-best title chance in the league, 28.7%, behind only Bear’s 29.3%.

Stuart

At the bottom, three teams at 3–10: Matt Gerardi, Brian Cross and Josh Martel, at 5.1%, 2.5% and 2.0%. Since 2021, when this league started playing thirteen games by Week 5, 8 teams have had three wins or fewer at this point. None made the playoffs.

Rich

That leaves the middle: Keith Creelman at 84.8%, Chris Klingenberg at 54.1%, Derrick Whitman at 39.9%, chasing the last two places. Klingenberg plays three games in Week 6, and all three are among the six with the most at stake. The numbers tell us who is pulling away. The rest of the show is why.

How these odds are made. Data through Week 5, Monday night included. Inputs fetched Tue Oct 13, 9:56 PM ET; 10,000 simulated seasons. Weeks 1–5 are fixed to the real results and the other 24 games of each team’s schedule are simulated from current rosters and weekly projections: RotoWire’s projected stat lines (via Sleeper) scored with UPS rules for offense, with MFL’s projections as the backup for anyone RotoWire does not project and for defenders, kickers and punters, and MFL deciding who plays. Each team keeps 70% of its projected edge over an average team, calibrated to UPS 2021–2025. Seeds follow the league rule: All-Play %, then overall record, then points. It does not know about trades or waiver moves after the cutoff, it starts every team’s best projected lineup, and it counts Questionable players as available (Rico Dowdle included). On MFL’s own offensive projections instead, four teams’ playoff odds move by ten points or more; RotoWire has tested as the more accurate source, so it is the one we print.

4 Who Is Winning Weeks? — edges over replacement and matchup swings
Stuart

Who is actually winning these weeks? Not just who scored. We measure each starter against a replacement-level player at his position that week: the average of the six rostered players just outside the league’s starting pool. Kickers and punters are left out.

Rich

The biggest edges this season: Jahmyr Gibbs, +125.0 for Brian Cutting; Josh Allen, +120.9 for Shawn Blake; Jaxon Smith-Njigba, +120.3 for Bear Dunn.

Stuart

A matchup swing is a win the player’s edge over replacement was bigger than the winning margin: put a replacement in his slot and that game flips. Two players lead the league with 4 each: Christian Watson for Keith Creelman and Tyler Shough for Derrick Whitman. Swings overlap, so they do not add up to wins.

Rich

Concentration risk: two teams lean hardest on two players. Shawn Blake’s quarterbacks, Josh Allen and Brock Purdy, are 32.2% of his team’s whole edge over replacement; Eric Mannila’s CeeDee Lamb and Bijan Robinson are 31.9% of his. Bear’s top two are 25.1%. Lose one of those players and Blake or Mannila loses more than anyone else would.

2. Pure Greatness: a 6–7 title contender

Stuart

Brian Cutting has the league’s most valuable player by our measure. Jahmyr Gibbs has 161.7 UPS points through Detroit’s first five games: 105 carries for 504 yards and 7 touchdowns, plus 27 catches. Score every running back since 1999 by this year’s UPS rules and only two started better: Priest Holmes in 2002 with 176.9, and Christian McCaffrey in 2019 with 166.0.

Rich

How unusual, and how likely to last? The 25 best five-game starts by running backs before this year kept about 80% of that pace for the rest of their seasons. At that rate he is a 25.9-a-game back instead of a 32.3-a-game one.

Stuart

So why 6–7? The lineups. In Week 5 Cutting started Deshaun Watson, who scored 10.5, while Tyler Huntley scored 29.8 and Aaron Rodgers 29.8 on his bench. Huntley came over with Lamar Jackson in the Sep 29 trade with Josh Martel, and Jackson did not play Sunday. Seven quarterbacks have been on Cutting’s roster in five weeks.

Rich

The forecast does not care who he started. It projects his best lineup every week, which is why he is 95.3% to make the playoffs and 28.7% for the title. Two things to watch: Detroit has a bye in Week 6, so no Gibbs, and Cutting plays Derrick Whitman, whom a loss would leave at 32.4%.

3. Bear Dunn’s receivers: Jaxon Smith-Njigba and Brock Bowers

Stuart

Bear started him for the last two of those games. The 36.0 in the first one came off his bench.

Stuart

What happens next: the 25 best five-game receiver starts before this year kept about 74% of their pace. Smith-Njigba plays Thursday night at Denver. And Bear is 83.1% for a first-round bye.

6 The Survival Line — what a Week 5 position has meant
Stuart

What has a Week 5 position meant in this league? We rebuilt every season’s standings as they stood after Week 5, using nothing from later in those seasons, and checked who made the playoffs. The comparable seasons are 2017 to 2025, the years with today’s format: four division winners and two wild cards, seeded by All-Play. Only 2021 on had thirteen head-to-head games by Week 5, like this year.

Rich

The finding: All-Play at Week 5 has sorted the eventual playoff teams from the rest a little better than record or points. Compare a playoff team with a non-playoff team from 2020 to 2025, and the one with the better All-Play was the playoff team 88% of the time; better record, 87%; more points, 85%.

Stuart

Since 2017, teams ranked 10th to 12th in All-Play after Week 5 have made it 4 times in 24. One of them was Bear Dunn in 2017, at 11–44 against the field, the exact line Josh Martel has today. That Bear team won its division, went in as the 6th seed and finished 3rd. It is the best comeback in the sample, and it is one.

Rich

And the hard line: since 2021, teams with three wins or fewer after Week 5 are 0 for 8. Gerardi, Cross and Josh Martel are on life support, not finished. Nobody is eliminated until the math says so.
