The Lineup

Quarterback is the one room that works, ranking 8. Matthew Stafford grades Low-end QB1 and Jared Goff High-end QB2 in the superflex. That is a functional superflex pair and it cost him most of his summer.

Everything else on offense is short. Receiver ranks 12, last in the league: Mike Evans is a Mid WR3, rookie Carnell Tate a Low-end WR3, Michael Wilson a High-end WR4 in the flex. The backfield ranks 11 on Kyren Williams at High-end RB2 and Rhamondre Stevenson at Mid RB3, with J.K. Dobbins, a Replacement-level RB, in the other flex. Travis Kelce is a Low-end TE1 and tight end ranks 10.

The defense has one real player and a lot of empty chairs. Marcus Jones grades Elite DB1 and is carried at the league minimum, which is the best thing on this roster by some margin. George Karlaftis is a Low-end DL2. After that: Chop Robinson and Abdul Carter both Replacement-level DL, Fred Warner a Replacement-level LB, and two starting slots filled by CJ Allen and Mansoor Delane, rookies no source can rate because neither has played an NFL season.

That is not this year's accident. 118 defensive acquisitions ranks 11, points per started defender ranks 12 — last in the league — and he put 2.8% of his auction money into defenders, the smallest share of any owner at 1 of 12. Three other teams built elite defensive fronts out of minimum-salary players this summer. He spent $3,000 and got what that buys.

Composite 12 of 12, 2 studs, 5 holes — the most in the league.

The Bridge

April was bleak. 19 active players, $142,000 committed, 7 of the priced slots empty of anybody the board ranks, and 0 top-twelve players anywhere on the roster. Legal-lineup value of 10,766 was the second-lowest in the league. The quarterback slot had nobody rostered in it at all.

Three months later, at the auction lock, almost nothing had changed: 21 active, value nudged to 11,470, holes still at 7, studs still at 0. Carnell Tate had replaced Michael Wilson at the second receiver slot and Wilson had moved into a flex. The quarterback slot was still empty.

Then the auction, which was the whole offseason compressed into a fortnight. Value went from 11,470 to 20,107 — very nearly double, the second-largest proportional jump in the league, behind only CBP. Matthew Stafford filled a quarterback slot that had been vacant since April and Jared Goff took the superflex off a wide receiver. Travis Kelce replaced Dalton Kincaid at tight end. Rhamondre Stevenson took the second back slot.

Four priced slots improved and studs went from 0 to 2. That is genuine progress and he should get credit for it. It also left 5 holes, which is still more than anyone else in the league, because he started so far behind that a good auction only got him to bad.

The Auctions

Nothing at the Expired Rookie Auction — 0 lots, $0 — from a pool of 14 players whose rookie deals ran out without a second one.

At the Free Agent Auction he won 11 lots for $106,000, and two of them were quarterbacks. Jared Goff cost $38,000, 35.8% of his budget, and Matthew Stafford cost nearly as much again. Between them those two took the overwhelming majority of what he had.

In isolation that is defensible — he arrived at the auction with no quarterback at all and superflex punishes that harder than any other gap. In context it is harder to love, because it meant nothing was left for a receiver room that ranks 12. Odell Beckham at the league minimum was his answer there, and he signed and released Beckham twice.

The value buys are real and worth naming. Travis Kelce at a small price is a Low-end TE1 and an upgrade on a bench player carrying five times the salary. Rhamondre Stevenson has already started and produced. Andres Borregales and Marcus Jones came in at the league minimum and both scored on opening night.

What he did not do was buy a defense. $3,000 across both auctions is the smallest defensive commitment in the league, and it is why two starting defensive slots are filled by rookies nobody can price.

Contracts, Trades, Cuts and the Rookie Class

6 contract moves, 1 trade, 5 removals, $0 charged this season and $2,000 deferred to next.

The contract sheet is all auction conversion: Goff, Stafford, Stevenson, Rachaad White, DeMarvion Overshown and Borregales all moved onto multi-year deals, and only Borregales is at the league minimum.

The single trade is small and good: a 2027 fourth to Real Deal Creel for George Karlaftis and a 2027 fifth. Karlaftis starts on the defensive line and is the second-best defender he owns.

Marquise Brown was the only real cut, carrying a penalty deferred to next season. The rest is 4 in-year flyers, two of them the same player.

The rookie class is his best asset and the reason not to write him off. 7 picks, led by Carnell Tate at 1.02 — one of the strongest historical slots in the class — who is already starting as a Low-end WR3. Makai Lemon at 1.06 and Kaelon Black at 2.02 came from decent bands and grade replacement-level so far. Two defenders at 4.02 and 6.12 are unrated by any source, which is not a verdict on them.

The Verdict

He is 3 of 3, behind C-Town Chivalry and Cleon Ca$h, and he plays both twice. C-Town is the best team in the league and Cleon Ca$h is fourth. Four of his games a year are against the two strongest opponents available to him, which is the worst divisional draw of anybody.

Strongest team in the league? No — 12 of 12, last, with the most holes and the worst receiver room. Best offseason? Also no, but he is not the worst either, and the distinction matters: he took the lowest pre-auction lineup value in the league and very nearly doubled it, filled a quarterback slot that had been empty since spring, and did it while still holding $26,000 of room — more than any other owner in the league. He is last, and he is last with money and a first-round rookie already starting.

The biggest advantage is Marcus Jones, an Elite DB1 at the league minimum, with Kyren Williams and the Stafford-Goff pair behind him. The biggest weakness is receiver, ranked 12, closely followed by a defense that ranks 12 historically and got $3,000 of investment this summer.

And then the strangest line in the league table. 2 NFL teams had played when this was written, and the last-ranked roster has the second-best haul of anybody: 24.8 points from three finished starters. Rhamondre Stevenson led it, with a minimum-salary kicker and a minimum-salary cornerback behind him. Nothing about one night predicts a season. But the cheap end of his roster is the part that has shown up so far, and on a team this short of money that is the best possible news.
