CBP Drafted, Waited, Then Spent to the Last Dollar

Derrick Whitman started April with 9 active players, made no trade, cut or signing before the auction beyond 6 rookie picks, then spent $172,000, most of it on Jonathan Taylor, D'Andre Swift, Kyle Pitts and the Tony Pollard that C-Town let go. He has $0 of cap room left, and the preseason simulation ranks CBP 7th.

Early Preseason

April was nearly empty: 9 active players, not enough for a lineup, and $132,000 in salary. Nico Collins was the one top-twelve player; Jordan Love, Emeka Egbuka and Garrett Wilson were solid; and behind them were only Keaton Mitchell at running back, Cole Kmet at tight end, Ricky Pearsall and Spencer Rattler.

Before the rookie draft he made no trade, no cut and no extension, and his one expiring rookie deal, A.T. Perry's, simply ran out.

Pre-Auction

The draft was the whole pre-auction offseason. Whitman made 6 picks, two in the first round: Kenyon Sadiq at 1.08, a slot where history finds a usable player a little under half the time, and Eli Stowers at 1.12, where it almost never has. Jonah Coleman at 2.01, Ted Hurst, Cade Klubnik and a defender, Keyshaun Elliott, followed. Sadiq and Coleman are already on the redraft board, both replacement-level for now.

No Expired Rookie Auction buys, no tags, no cuts, no trades. He went into the auction with 12 active players and $150,000 in active-roster salary: plenty of room, and plenty of rebuilding to do with it.

The Auction and Preseason Contracts

CBP spent $172,000 at the Free Agent Auction on 16 players, and made a little more room first. On the auction's opening weekend Jordan Love was extended one more year, $31,000 for 2027, and restructured the same day, back-loaded to $11,000 this year and $41,000 next. Then Whitman bought a backfield: Jonathan Taylor for $55,000, D'Andre Swift for $26,000 and Tony Pollard, cut by C-Town on lock day, for $28,000 after thirteen bids. Kyle Pitts cost $25,000.

What it bought was real: Taylor and Pitts, a High-end RB1 and a High-end TE1, the two Very good buys; Swift, the Good one; and Pollard, who starts. Aaron Jones and Michael Pittman are bench depth. His offense and C-Town's improved the most at the auction, all but tied; the league table ranks CBP 2nd of 12, by a hair.

The contracts that followed ran him to the cap. Pitts signed front-loaded, $35,000 this year and $15,000 in 2027. During the auction Whitman cut Nico Collins's salary to $19,000 for a temporary $11,000 of bidding room, and 25 days later, the day the deal was put back, he took Jayden Reed, three depth players and $12,000 of blind-bid money from Blake Bombers for Pearsall, Coleman and a 2027 fourth, a B from the trade bot. He cut Kmet at no cost, claimed Calvin Ridley and Xavier Hutchinson off waivers, and sits at $0 of room.

The lineup it built has 3 top-twelve starters, Taylor, Collins and Pitts, five solid ones around them, and 1 hole, Pollard in a flex. Quarterback is the soft spot: Tyler Shough and Love both grade as a Mid QB2, and his quarterbacks rank 11th in the league. Graded on MFL's projections the defense is thin, Foyesade Oluokun the only defender above replacement level.

No starter is hurt. The injury list is all bench and taxi: Keaton Mitchell and Eli Stowers are questionable, and Isiah Pacheco and Dillon Gabriel are on injured reserve into October.

History and the Division

Whitman's teams have had the league's best all-play record 4 times, in 2013, 2014, 2015, 2023, and won 7 division titles, but the championship count is one, in 2013, with a runner-up finish in 2024. His career all-play record is 60.0%.

This year's division is one he built. Whitman sat 2nd among the four captains, took Gride 2nd overall and came back for Blake Bombers 7th, and he has made a point of calling LEGION OF BOOM his division, not ours, because he put it together, though in July he picked Blake to win it. The simulation agrees: Blake Bombers are the favorite at 52.2%, CBP is at 36.0% with 52.0% playoff odds, and Gride is at 11.8%.

The division weeks are 1, 4, 8, 11 and 14, and one of them is a problem. Week eight costs him three starters, Shough, Collins and Kaden Elliss, and Gride and Blake Bombers three apiece, George Kittle and Brock Purdy among Blake's. But week eleven takes six CBP starters at once, Pitts, Love and four defenders, against four apiece for his rivals. Week fourteen, the last of the regular season, has no byes at all in the division. Across the five weeks he loses 9 starters to byes, Gride 7 and Blake Bombers 7.

The next three years: in 2027 the young players grow up; in 2028 CBP makes the playoffs and finally solves quarterback at an auction; in 2029 it wins the division he put together himself.

Week one is a division week and has barely touched CBP: with 4 NFL teams done, Leonard Williams had a sack and CBP has 10.0 points in the bank.
