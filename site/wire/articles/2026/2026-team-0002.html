The Lineup

Jonathan Taylor is an Elite RB1 and the reason this roster functions. D'Andre Swift is a Low-end RB2 beside him and the backfield ranks 5, which is the best thing on the sheet. Kyle Pitts grades High-end TE1 and lands the tight end room at 5 — a genuine positional edge in a league where most teams are starting somebody they do not want to name out loud.

Receiver holds up too. Nico Collins is a Low-end WR1, Emeka Egbuka a Mid WR2, Garrett Wilson a Low-end WR2 in the flex, and the room ranks 7. Three real starters and no passengers.

Then quarterback, which ranks 11 and is the entire argument against this team. Tyler Shough grades Mid QB2 and Jordan Love grades Mid QB2. That is not a superflex pair, it is the same player twice. In a format that forces you to start two of them, going into a season with a matched set of backups is a decision, and it is the wrong one.

The defense is honest work and no more. Jared Verse is a High-end DL2 and Kaden Elliss a High-end LB2. Behind them Grant Delpit and Kyle Dugger are both Replacement-level DB, and Edgerrin Cooper — who cost real money — fills the last flex as a Replacement-level LB. Historically this is not his game: 166 defensive acquisitions ranks 10, and points per started defender ranks 10.

The composite lands 9 of 12 with 3 studs and only 1 hole. That gap between rank and hole count is the whole team in one line: there is a competent body in nearly every slot and almost nothing that wins a week by itself.

The Bridge

In April this roster held 9 active players. Not thin — nearly empty, the barest in the league, with $132,000 still committed to them. Contracts expire here and rosters empty out, so that is the cycle rather than a scandal, but the depth of it mattered: he had further to climb than anyone.

He barely started before the auction. At the roster lock he was at 12 active and still could not legally field a lineup. Tyler Shough had replaced Jordan Love at the top of the quarterback slot, Kenyon Sadiq had replaced Cole Kmet at tight end, and that was the offseason to that point. Legal-lineup value had moved from 13,117 to 16,127, which is to say barely.

Everything happened at the auction. He came out at 28 active and 29,800 of lineup value, with holes cut from 5 to 1 and studs up from 1 to 3. Jonathan Taylor arrived on top of Keaton Mitchell at the first back slot, D'Andre Swift filled a second back slot that had literally nobody in it, and Kyle Pitts took tight end off Sadiq. Four of the priced slots changed occupant in a fortnight.

It is a real rebuild and it deserves credit. It also finished with $0 of room against $300,000 committed, which means the fix for the quarterback room does not exist unless somebody leaves.

The Auctions

He did not buy a single player at the Expired Rookie Auction — 0 lots, $0 spent, out of a pool of 14 players league-wide. That is a defensible pass rather than an oversight: the ERA sells men whose rookie deals ran out without earning a second one, and its most expensive player anywhere went for $7,000.

The Free Agent Auction was the opposite of a pass. 16 lots, $172,000, and a clear obsession. Jonathan Taylor at $55,000 — 32.0% of his auction money and a bargain against the $88,000 somebody else paid for a top price elsewhere. Then Tony Pollard, then D'Andre Swift, then Aaron Jones, then Isiah Pacheco. Five running backs.

Taylor and Swift start. Pollard is the third-most expensive player he bought all summer and he grades Replacement-level RB in the flex. Aaron Jones and Pacheco sit on the bench, both replacement-level, one of them at a price that would have bought a starting quarterback. Kyle Pitts at tight end was the smartest buy of his auction and the running back pile-up is what he spent instead of solving the position ranked 11.

The defensive shopping was quick and cheap: Edgerrin Cooper and Foyesade Oluokun with a run of minimum-priced bodies behind them, $18,000 in total, 10.5% of his money, ranking 6 of 12 on that share.

Contracts, Trades, Cuts and the Rookie Class

5 contract moves, 1 trade, 1 roster removal, $0 of penalty. This is a quiet ledger attached to a loud auction.

Jordan Love got an extension and then a restructure in the same sitting — two different operations on one contract, not one move described twice. Kyle Pitts and Michael Pittman were converted to multi-year deals off their auction wins, and neither is at the league minimum whatever the Pittman figure looks like.

The interesting one is Nico Collins, restructured and then reverted, which freed $11,000 of temporary room at exactly the moment he needed it and put the contract back afterwards. That is a legal and well-timed piece of cap handling, and it is the only genuinely clever thing on this sheet.

The single trade went to Blake Bombers, who share his division and finished ahead of him. He sent Ricky Pearsall, Jonah Coleman and a 2027 fourth, and got back Jayden Reed, three players the redraft board does not price at all, and $12,000 of blind-bid money. Reed is the only piece with a value on him and he grades Replacement-level WR on the bench at a salary that says he was supposed to be more. Selling a second-round rookie pick to a division rival for cash and a bench receiver is not obviously a win.

One cut: Cole Kmet, removed to make room, no penalty charged. Nothing else left.

The rookie class had two genuinely good slots and he has not converted them yet. Kenyon Sadiq at 1.08 sits in the best historical band of anyone's class this year and grades Replacement-level TE so far. Jonah Coleman at 2.01 was in the second-best band and has already been traded. Cade Klubnik at 4.03 is a lottery ticket, and Keyshaun Elliott at 6.02 is a defender neither the board nor the production model can price yet — no opinion, not a bad one.

The Verdict

His division has 3 teams and he sits 2 in it, behind Blake Bombers and ahead of Gride. He plays both of them twice, which makes the Bombers trade look worse than it looked in August, and makes Gride the game he has to hold.

Strongest team in the league? No: 9 of 12. Best offseason, though — that is a genuinely different question and he has a real case. He took a roster of 9 bodies that could not field a lineup and turned it into one with 1 hole in it. Almost nobody in this league moved that far.

The biggest advantage is Jonathan Taylor with Kyle Pitts behind him, an Elite RB1 and a High-end TE1 at two positions where most rosters are compromising. The biggest weakness is quarterback, ranked 11, where Tyler Shough and Jordan Love are the same grade as each other and he has $0 left to do anything about it.

For what one afternoon is worth: 2 NFL teams had played when this was written, and the only CBP starter among them was Leonard Williams, bought at the league minimum, who banked 10.0 points with a sack in it. The cheapest player in his auction was the first one to pay.
