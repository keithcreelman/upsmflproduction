The Bridge

April found him already in decent shape: 18 active, able to field a legal lineup, 5 studs and 2 holes, worth 33,566 with $231,000 committed. One of those holes was literal — the second running back slot had nobody rostered in it at all.

That slot is where his offseason actually happened, and it happened at the draft rather than the auction. Jeremiyah Love arrived at 1.01, the strongest historical slot in the entire class, and walked straight into the lineup as a High-end RB2. By the auction lock, value had climbed to 37,248 and holes were down to 1 — before a single auction bid was placed.

Then the auction itself, and this is the number that defines his year. Lineup value went from 37,248 to 37,252. That is a change of four points. Studs did not move from 5, holes did not move from 1, and the only priced slot to change occupant swapped one Replacement-level WR for another.

He filled the roster out to 32 active players and the starting lineup came out the other side statistically identical. For a team ranked 4 that is either admirable restraint or a missed window, and which one depends entirely on whether you think the defense described above will hold up.

The Auctions

Nothing at the Expired Rookie Auction: 0 lots, $0, from a pool of 14 players whose rookie deals ran out without earning a second one.

At the Free Agent Auction he won 13 lots for $34,000, the smallest outlay of any owner in the league. Romeo Doubs took $15,000 of it — 44.1% of his budget, against a league-high lot elsewhere of $88,000 — and Doubs grades Replacement-level WR and is the single weak link in his starting lineup. Twelve bids went in on him. That is the most contested lot he entered and it is the one purchase he should want back.

Kirk Cousins and Tyler Allgeier were modest additions who both grade replacement-level on the bench. Everything else came in at the league minimum: Keon Coleman, Antoine Winfield, Tyler Nubin, Teddye Buchanan, Carl Granderson, Jonathan Greenard, T.J. Edwards.

The defensive spend was $6,000, 17.6% of his money, ranking 9 of 12 by share. Three of those minimum-salary defenders are now starting and all three grade replacement-level. Buying cheap defenders worked beautifully for three other owners this summer; here it produced exactly what it cost.

Contracts, Trades, Cuts and the Rookie Class

6 contract moves, 1 trade, 3 removals, $0 of penalty charged and nothing deferred. This is the quietest sheet in the league.

The contract work is genuinely good. Bijan Robinson was extended in May and then restructured in July, two separate operations that between them cut his current-year charge to a fraction of the extension figure while leaving the total value untouched. Justin Herbert was restructured in June and front-loaded. Tucker Kraft was extended. Those three deals are why an owner carrying an Elite RB1, a Mid QB1, a High-end WR1 and an Elite TE1 still had room to operate.

The single trade of his year was a 2027 fifth-round pick to Sex Manther for Efton Chism, a receiver the redraft board does not price. It is not a story.

The removals are three players and no consequences: one contract cut and 2 in-year flyers, none of them charged against the cap.

The draft is where he won. 7 picks, and the first of them is the best single selection anybody made this year — Jeremiyah Love at 1.01, from the strongest historical band in the class, already starting as a High-end RB2. Zachariah Branch at 4.10 came from a slot where history says almost nothing usable emerges and already grades Mid WR1, which makes him the steal of the draft. Arvell Reese at 3.07 is starting at linebacker with no NFL season behind him — no source can price him, which is not a criticism of the player so much as a description of what is behind him.

The Verdict

He is 2 of 3, behind C-Town Chivalry and ahead of the Hawks, and he plays both twice. C-Town is the best team in the league, which means his divisional draw is two games against the top-ranked roster and two against the bottom-ranked one. The Hawks games are the cushion; the C-Town games are the season.

Strongest team in the league? Fourth, and closer to the top than the gap suggests. Best offseason? No — and it is worth saying plainly that he did not have one. He spent the least of anybody, made one trade for a player nobody rates, and moved his starting lineup by four points of value across the entire auction. What he did have was the first pick in the draft and the sense to extend and restructure his best contracts properly.

The biggest advantage is the two tight ends and Bijan Robinson: first in the league at a position everybody else is compromising at, plus an Elite RB1. The biggest weakness is a defensive front of three replacement-level linemen and a rookie linebacker, on a roster that has never invested there and ranks 12 all-time for trying.
