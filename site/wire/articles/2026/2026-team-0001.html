Early Preseason

April was thin and cheap. Bousquet carried 15 active players, not enough for a full lineup, and only $84,000 in salary. Chase Brown was the one top-twelve player, and Bo Nix and Jake Ferguson were solid. The rest were stopgaps: Zach Charbonnet and Tank Bigsby, whose rookie deals had just run out, Alec Pierce, Courtland Sutton, Jauan Jennings and Carson Wentz.

The early moves were about money. In April David Montgomery and his $12,000 salary went to HammerTime for a 2027 second; the trade bot did not grade deals yet, and graded now as of that day it comes out A for L.A. Looks. In May Brown's rookie deal was extended for $22,000 a year for 2 years, a TCV of $44,000. Brown was one of 5 rookie deals running out; 4 of the others went to the Expired Rookie Auction, where Bousquet bought Charbonnet and Bigsby back and let Brian Branch and Michael Mayer go to C-Town and Real Deal Creel.

Pre-Auction

The tags were the story. Bousquet tagged Kamren Curl at $4,000 and Amon-Ra St. Brown at $51,000 in May, and within two months he had traded St. Brown. In July St. Brown went to Gride with Shedeur Sanders and $10,000 of blind-bid money for Sam Darnold, Jalen Coker and Emmett Johnson, moving $53,000 of salary out and $24,000 in, and the trade bot graded his side a B-. A top receiver on a one-year tag became a starting quarterback and room for the auction.

Before any of that, the rookie draft and the Expired Rookie Auction in late May filled out the roster. On draft day he traded 3.08 to HammerTime for 4.08, 5.01 and a 2027 fourth, a B- from the bot, then made 6 picks, starting with Bryce Lance at 3.10. At the Expired Rookie Auction he paid $7,000 to keep Charbonnet and $4,000 for Bigsby, and bought Jordan Battle and Marvin Mims. In July he sent Battle, Adonai Mitchell and his 2027 fifth to The Long Haulers for Isaiah Bond and their 2027 fourth. The bot first graded that an A+ for him, then posted a correction the next morning: it had run the trade backwards, and his side was a C. No cuts. The roster went into the auction at 21 active players and $137,000 in salary.

The Auction and Preseason Contracts

L.A. Looks spent $123,000 at the Free Agent Auction on 14 players, and the biggest single price went on one receiver: George Pickens, whom C-Town had tagged and then released on lock day, cost $48,000 after eleven bids. Daniel Jones cost $24,000 and Jaylen Warren $20,000.

What it bought: Pickens, a Mid WR1 and the one Very good buy; Jones, a Good one, in a superflex league where most lineups carry two quarterbacks; and Warren, a Low-end RB3 who starts. Of the rest of the offensive buys, only Isaiah Likely made the lineup, as a replacement-level tight end in a flex, and the league table ranks the auction 7th of 12 for how much it improved his offense.

The contracts came late, days before the opener. Pickens signed for 3 years at $48,000 a year, a TCV of $144,000, and two of his Expired Rookie Auction buys got multi-year deals too: Charbonnet, a TCV of $21,000, and Mims at the league minimum. Alec Pierce was extended one more year: $2,000 this year and $12,000 in 2027, a TCV of $14,000. Darnold's deal was restructured after the auction, front-loaded to $30,000 this year and $16,000 in 2027, and in early September so was Chase Brown's: front-loaded to $32,000 this year and $12,000 in 2027. Bousquet also claimed Chris Brooks off waivers in early September and released Wentz, Riley Leonard and Troy Franklin at no cost, and he is down to $5,000 of room.

The lineup it built has 2 top-twelve starters, Brown and Pickens, with Nix, Ferguson and Darnold solid, and 4 holes: Warren at the second running back spot, Pierce at receiver, Sutton in a flex and Isaiah Likely, a replacement-level tight end, in the other. The defense, graded on MFL's projections, is strong up front: Laiatu Latu and Zack Baun are top-twelve starters at their positions, Danielle Hunter and Jamien Sherwood are a tier behind, and both safeties are replacement-level.

The injury that matters is at quarterback. Darnold was sacked on Seattle's first drive of the opener, left with a hip injury and did not return; a fracture was ruled out, but NFL Network reports he is expected to miss week two. MFL has not listed him yet, so the forecast holds him out of that week by hand, and Daniel Jones, the auction backup, starts in the superflex. Charbonnet is on injured reserve into October. Aaron Donald, a Low-end DL3 on MFL's projections, is not hurt; he is listed out for the first two weeks on a coach's decision.

History and the Division

Bousquet is an original, here since 2010, and the record is a winner's: titles in 2014, 2021, runner-up finishes in 2015, 2023, 11 playoff trips in 16 seasons, and 5 division titles. He has never had the league's best all-play record, though he has finished second in it three times. His career all-play record is 54.9%.

His new division was built on a rivalry pick. This is year one of the alignment the owners picked themselves, and Josh Martel, the last of the four captains, took Real Deal Creel 4th overall and doubled back for L.A. Looks 5th, rivals on purpose. Maddie Mondays is the closest division in the simulation: Real Deal Creel 40.1%, Sex Manther 37.7% and L.A. Looks 22.2%, with 34.0% playoff odds for Bousquet.

The division weeks are 1, 4, 8, 11 and 14, and the byes run against him late. Week eight costs him Likely and Hunter, and Sex Manther only its kicker. Week eleven takes Darnold and Curl, while Real Deal Creel loses Puka Nacua, Christian Watson and TreVeyon Henderson. Week fourteen, the last week of the regular season and a division week, takes three Cowboys at once, Pickens, Ferguson and Brandon Aubrey, while Sex Manther loses nobody. Across the five weeks he loses 7 starters to byes, Real Deal Creel 6 and Sex Manther 4.

The next three years, as a memo to his Esteemed Colleagues. 2027: a building year, and he would like the record to show it was a choice. 2028: Pickens is a top receiver, and Maddie Mondays is informed, at length, in the group chat. 2029: the division title, accepted with a movie quote and a GIF, and a note that the third championship is next on the agenda.

Week one is a division week, and Darnold's part of it is already over: with 4 NFL teams done, L.A. Looks has 3.1 points in the bank, almost none of them from Darnold, whose game ended on the first drive.
