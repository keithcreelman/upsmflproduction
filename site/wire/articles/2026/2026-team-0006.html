Early Preseason

April found the Long Haulers top-heavy. Cross carried 20 active players and $270,000 in salary, with 5 real starters on offense: Jalen Hurts, a High-end QB1, Derrick Henry at running back, A.J. Brown at receiver, Tyler Warren, a High-end TE1, and Trevor Lawrence in the superflex. The 4 holes were Jordan Mason at the second running back spot, Josh Downs at the second receiver spot, and Tre Tucker and Woody Marks in the two flexes, all replacement-level.

He did his shopping before the draft and paid in picks, not salary. In April his 2027 first- and second-round picks went to HammerTime for Hurts, and HammerTime added $20,000 of blind-bid money to the deal, so Cross was paid to take on a $67,000 quarterback. In May a 2027 third bought Quentin Johnston and his $18,000 salary from Pure Greatness, and five later-round rookie picks went to Sex Manther for 3.04 and 3.06. Before the draft he took on $85,000 of salary in trades and sent none away.

There were no cuts and no extensions in that stretch. Two rookie deals ran out: Devon Witherspoon is still on the roster, and Will Levis went to the Expired Rookie Auction, where Cross bought him back.

Pre-Auction

On draft day he flipped 2.03 and the 3.06 he had just bought to Gride for 2.05 and 2.10, then made 7 picks: Fernando Mendoza at 1.03, a slot that has produced a usable player more often than not, then Malachi Fields, Germie Bernard, Mike Washington Jr., Carson Beck at 3.04, Kevin Coleman and Keldric Faulk. Mendoza and Beck make two rookie quarterbacks behind Hurts and Lawrence. None of the class grades as a starter yet, which at this stage is the expectation, not a verdict.

At the Expired Rookie Auction he bought Tyjae Spears for $1,000 and Levis back for $1,000. He tagged nobody. His cuts before the lock were small: six taxi-squad and minimum-salary bodies at no cost, and one real release, Theo Johnson in July, with $5,000 of salary and a $1,250 penalty. A July trade with L.A. Looks sent Isaiah Bond and a 2027 fourth for safety Jordan Battle, Adonai Mitchell and a 2027 fifth, and on the day the auction roster locked he extended Hurts at $52,000 this year and $119,000 in all.

The Auction and Preseason Contracts

Then Cross spent the Free Agent Auction on defense. Of his $37,000, $28,000 went to defenders: Maxx Crosby for $8,000 and Brian Burns for $7,000, both Elite DL1s, and Jordyn Brooks, an Elite LB1, for $4,000. It is a real defense, and like every defense in this league it is a footnote to the offense.

On offense he spent $7,000, and that is why the league table flatters him. It ranks value gained per dollar, and so little money that moved the lineup at all ranks high: 3rd of 12. The What it bought column is the honest one. Three depth players, George Holani, Jalen Nailor and Nick Mullens, and in total value added he was 10th of 12.

The contract work ran through the auction. The lock-day restructure cut Hurts to $47,000 for 2026 without touching his $119,000 total, a little room opened for bidding. 6 auction buys and both Expired Rookie Auction buys got multi-year contracts, and so did two trade pickups, Jordan Battle and Gunnar Helm. One of those did not last: Levis, bought back and signed on, was cut in August with a $1,000 penalty that lands on the 2027 cap. Josh Downs was extended in September at $12,000 this year and $34,000 in all.

After the auction came the churn: 9 pickups before week one, most of them tight ends passing through. Zach Ertz, Darren Waller and Matthew Hibner all came and went, Helm arrived by trade, and Najee Harris and Malik Davis joined at running back. The 13 releases in that stretch cost $3,000 in penalties, all of it charged to 2027.

The lineup it built has the shape it had in April: 5 real starters in Hurts, Henry, Brown, Warren and Lawrence, and 4 holes, now George Holani at the second running back spot, Quentin Johnston at the second receiver spot, and Jordan Mason and Josh Downs in the flexes. The kicker, Jason Myers, led all kickers in points per game last year and cost $1,000.

Historical Context

Cross is a short story so far. The league's documented record starts him in 2025, and his one completed season produced a 39.0% all-play record and no playoff trip. No titles, no division crowns, nothing on the wall yet, which is the easiest kind of history to improve on.

The Division

Outlast PG Time is the division that holds the last two champions, and Cross plays each of them five times: Pure Greatness won in 2025 and HammerTime in 2024. The simulation likes them again, Pure Greatness 2nd with 14.7% title odds and HammerTime 4th. It puts the Long Haulers 9th, with 40.8% playoff odds and a 20.9% chance at the division.

The next three years, filed the way Cross would file them. 2027: no first-round pick, because HammerTime owns it, and he congratulates HammerTime by name when they use it. The defense he bought this summer is still under contract and still the best thing on the depth chart. 2028: Mendoza or Beck takes the superflex from Lawrence, the Team finally fills the second receiver spot, and it is Let's GO season in the group chat. 2029, and this is a prediction, not a verdict: the Fantasy Gods square the account, and a Championship, capital C, ships to the Long Haulers.

Week one has barely started, with 4 NFL teams done. Myers kicked two field goals, A.J. Brown caught three passes, and the Long Haulers have 17.1 points in the bank.
