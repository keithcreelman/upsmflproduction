The Bridge

In April he had 21 active players, 6 studs — the most in the league at that point — and $277,000 committed. He could field a legal lineup and it was a good one, worth 29,967.

Then he took it apart. By the auction lock the active roster was unchanged at 21, but committed salary had collapsed to $103,000. Studs fell from 6 to four, holes rose from 3 to four, and legal-lineup value dropped from 29,967 to 22,223. On paper that is the worst three months anybody had.

It was deliberate, and the slot table shows what it cost him in the moment. Christian McCaffrey vacated the first back slot and Achane slid up into it. George Kittle left the first offensive flex. Jonathon Brooks, a Replacement-level RB, ended up in the superflex — a running back occupying the slot a second quarterback should hold, which is what a roster looks like mid-demolition.

The auction put it back together and then some: 35 active, 34,171 of value — higher than where he started in April — with holes back down to 2. Lamar Jackson took the top quarterback slot and pushed Caleb Williams into the superflex, which is the single best pair of moves in one room anybody made. Jaylen Waddle and Jameson Williams filled two more.

He finished the offseason stronger than he started it while spending three months looking like he was quitting. That is the whole trick and almost nobody else attempted it.

The Auctions

One lot at the Expired Rookie Auction, Sean Tucker for $4,000, from a pool of 14 players whose rookie deals ran out without a second one. He has since been discarded.

The Free Agent Auction is where the money went: 14 lots for $150,000. Lamar Jackson alone took $62,000, which is 41.3% of his auction spend and, not by coincidence, the identical figure he had been paying Christian McCaffrey. He swapped a running back's salary for an Elite QB1 at the same price. Jackson drew more bids than any other lot he entered and he won him anyway.

Jaylen Waddle and Jameson Williams were sensible follow-ups. Talanoa Hufanga at a small price is the best value on the sheet, an Elite DB1 for less than most owners paid for a punter.

The mistake is Kenneth Gainwell, bought for real money and now sitting on the bench as a Replacement-level RB while a Replacement-level RB starts ahead of him in the lineup. That is the one lot where the plan and the outcome do not meet, and it is at the position ranked 9.

Defensively he spent $8,000 in total — Van Ginkel, Hufanga, Demetrius Knight — and filled the rest of the unit with rookies. It is the cheapest defense in the league and it shows in the grades.

Contracts, Trades, Cuts and the Rookie Class

9 contract moves, 5 trades, 11 removals. The removals are the headline and they are savage.

Christian McCaffrey was tagged in May at one of the largest salaries in the competition, and cut in July with no penalty at all — the full salary came straight back onto the cap. Three days later Tyreek Hill went the same way at the same salary, this time with a penalty attached, and Tanner McKee with him. Between them those two releases are why $27,500 of cap penalty is sitting in the ledger with no charge year stamped on it — the largest unstamped figure of any team, and a bill that is going to land somewhere.

Tagging a player in May and releasing him in July is, on its face, an expensive way to change your mind. In this case the tag cost nothing to walk away from and the money became Lamar Jackson, so the sequence works. It is still worth saying plainly that he paid to keep a player he then decided he did not want.

The contract sheet is busy and well judged. Rashee Rice and De'Von Achane were both extended in June, and both were later restructured — Achane's current-year charge cut sharply, Rice's raised. Those are four separate operations on two contracts, and together they are what made the auction budget possible.

The trades are all future-facing. He sold 2026 pick 1.10 to Blake Bombers for a 2027 first before the draft even started, sent George Kittle and $5,000 of blind-bid money to the same partner for a 2027 second, and turned two mid-round picks into five from The Long Haulers in May.

That haul is also the weakness of the class. 7 picks, not one earlier than 3.11, and four of them defenders no source can price yet. Volume, at slots where history says volume is mostly what you get.

The Verdict

He is 1 of 3 in his division, ahead of Real Deal Creel and L.A. Looks, and he plays both twice. That is the most valuable position on this sheet: he leads the division outright, and both teams chasing him sit below him in the league table as well.

Strongest team in the league? Not quite — 5 of 12. Best offseason? He has as strong a claim as anybody. He removed two of the largest contracts in the competition, took a short-term hit that made his roster look like a fire sale in July, and came out of the auction with more lineup value than he had in April and the best quarterback room in the league.

The biggest advantage is Lamar Jackson and Caleb Williams, an Elite QB1 and a High-end QB1 in the same lineup. The biggest weakness is everything behind De'Von Achane at running back, with a defense of unknowns and replacement-level bodies close behind.

His starter among them was Julian Love, bought at the league minimum, who intercepted a pass and banked 15.3 points, one of the best returns anybody got that night and comfortably the best per dollar.
