Sex Manther Tore Down a Contender in July and Rebuilt a Better Offense

Josh Martel went into the auction carrying $103,000 of salary, down from $277,000 in April. He released Christian McCaffrey and Tyreek Hill in the same week and used the money on one of the two best quarterback rooms in the league.

The Lineup

Lamar Jackson grades Elite QB1 and Caleb Williams High-end QB1. In a superflex format that rewards a second starting quarterback, that is the pairing everybody else is trying to build, and the room ranks 2nd in the league.

Receiver ranks 5th and is deep rather than top-heavy: Rashee Rice at Low-end WR1, Jaylen Waddle at Low-end WR2, Jameson Williams at Low-end WR2 in a flex and Rome Odunze at High-end WR3 in the other. Harold Fannin is a Mid TE1 and tight end ranks 6th.

The backfield is the weakest room and it is a real problem, ranked 9th. De'Von Achane grades Mid RB1 and behind him is Jonathon Brooks, a Replacement-level RB, starting. Kenneth Gainwell, the third-most expensive player he bought all summer, is a Replacement-level RB on the bench.

Defense is where he saved money. He put 5.2% of his auction money into defenders, the second-smallest share in the league, and two of the seven defenders in his strongest lineup, Jaishawn Barham and Dillon Thieneman, have not played an NFL season yet.

The Bridge

In April he had 6 studs — tied with C-Town for the most in the league at that point — only 3 holes, and $277,000 committed. It was a contender's roster.

Then he took it apart. By the auction lock committed salary had collapsed to $103,000. Two of his 6 studs were gone, and a fourth hole had opened. No team's starting offense fell further over those three months.

It was deliberate, and it cost him in the moment. Christian McCaffrey vacated the first back slot and Achane slid up into it, leaving Jonathon Brooks, a Replacement-level RB, in the second. George Kittle left the first offensive flex. And the superflex, the spot a second quarterback should hold, held a Replacement-level RB, Jacory Croskey-Merritt, in April and a Replacement-level TE, Oronde Gadsden, at the lock.

The auction put it back together. When it closed his studs were back up to 5 and his holes down to 2, and his starting offense was more than half again as good as the one he took into it — and better than the one he had in April. Lamar Jackson took the top quarterback slot and pushed Caleb Williams into the superflex, one purchase that upgraded two slots. Jaylen Waddle and Jameson Williams filled two more.

He finished the offseason with a better offense than he started it with.

The Auctions

One buy at the Expired Rookie Auction, Sean Tucker for $4,000, one of 14 players sold there after their rookie deals ran out without a second one. He has since been discarded.

The Free Agent Auction is where the money went: 14 players for $150,000. Lamar Jackson alone took $62,000, which is 41.3% of that money and the identical figure he had been paying Christian McCaffrey. He swapped a running back's tag salary for an Elite QB1 at the same figure, on a back-loaded deal that charges him less than that this year. Jackson drew more bids than anyone else he bought, and Martel won him anyway.

Measured against the money, it was good business rather than great. He put $138,000 into offense at the Free Agent Auction, and for what that money bought he ranks 5th of 12. C-Town Chivalry spent the identical sum and got more for it, and Blake Bombers got more while spending less.

Jaylen Waddle and Jameson Williams were sensible follow-ups. The mistake is Kenneth Gainwell, bought for real money and now sitting on the bench as a Replacement-level RB while a Replacement-level RB starts ahead of him in the lineup. That is the costliest buy where the plan and the outcome do not meet, and it is at the position ranked 9th.

Defense got $8,000 across both auctions, one of the smallest defensive budgets in the league, spread over Andrew Van Ginkel, Talanoa Hufanga and Demetrius Knight.

Contracts, Trades, Cuts and the Rookie Class

9 contract moves, 5 trades, 11 removals. The removals are the headline and they are savage.

Christian McCaffrey was tagged in May and cut in July with no penalty at all — the full salary came straight off his cap. Three days later Tyreek Hill went the same way at the same salary, this time with a penalty attached, and Tanner McKee with him. Between them, Hill's and McKee's releases are why $27,500 of cap penalty has no charge year yet — the largest such figure of any team, and a bill that is going to land somewhere.

The tag cost him nothing to walk away from, and the money became Lamar Jackson.

The contract sheet is busy and well judged. Rashee Rice and De'Von Achane were both extended in June, and both were later restructured. Achane's came the day before the auction lock and cut his current-year charge sharply, which is part of what paid for the auction; Rice's came a month after the auction closed and raised his. Those are four separate operations on two contracts.

The trades are all future-facing. He sold 2026 pick 1.10 to Blake Bombers for a 2027 first before the draft even started, sent George Kittle and $5,000 of blind-bid money to the same partner for a 2027 second, and turned two mid-round picks into five from The Long Haulers in May.

That haul is also the weakness of the class. 7 picks, not one earlier than 3.11, and four of them defenders with no NFL season behind them yet. Volume, at slots where history says volume is mostly what you get.

The Verdict

He is 1st of 3 in his division, ahead of Real Deal Creel and L.A. Looks, and he plays each of them five times, starting this week. No division game has been decided yet, so that order is the power rankings rather than the standings, but both teams chasing him start the season below him.

Strongest team in the league? No — 5th of 12. Boldest offseason? Nobody took more salary off his roster before the auction. He cut Christian McCaffrey and Tyreek Hill, let his starting offense fall further than anyone's before the auction, and came out of it with a better one than he had in April and one of the two best quarterback rooms in the league.

The biggest advantage is Lamar Jackson and Caleb Williams, an Elite QB1 and a High-end QB1 in the same lineup. The biggest weakness is everything behind De'Von Achane at running back.

2 NFL teams had played when this was written. His starter among them was Julian Love, who intercepted a pass and banked 15.3 points, the second-best return by any rostered player that night, on a minimum contract. One game, from one defender, and a long way from a verdict.
