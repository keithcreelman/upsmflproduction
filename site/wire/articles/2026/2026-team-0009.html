The Bridge

In April he had 17 active players, which is too few for a legal lineup, but 6 of the priced slots already held a top-twelve player and only 1 did not. He was carrying $215,000 and 38,002 of lineup value. The quality was already there.

Between April and the auction lock he went backwards on purpose, down to 15 active and $137,000 committed — a drop of real size — while value slipped to 35,096. Drake London left the second receiver slot and Tee Higgins moved up into it. Tua Tagovailoa, a Replacement-level QB, ended up in the superflex. For a fortnight this was the roster of a team in trouble.

The auction ended that in a single afternoon. 34 active, 48,616 of lineup value, 0 holes. Christian McCaffrey took the first back slot and pushed James Cook to the second, which pushed Breece Hall into a flex, which pushed Tetairoa McMillan into the other one. Joe Burrow took quarterback and moved Jayden Daniels into the superflex.

One auction moved five of the priced slots and turned a roster that could not field a lineup into the highest lineup value in the league. Nobody else came close to that swing.

The Auctions

One lot at the Expired Rookie Auction — Brian Branch for $4,000, out of a pool of 14 players whose rookie deals ran out without earning a second one.

At the Free Agent Auction he took 19 lots for $152,000, and the shape of it is stark. Christian McCaffrey cost $63,000, 41.4% of the total, and drew more bids than any other lot in the auction. Joe Burrow cost nearly as much again. Those two players took the overwhelming majority of his budget.

McCaffrey is worth pausing on. Sex Manther had tagged him in May and released him in July, at which point he became the most contested player of the summer and landed here for a price barely above what that tag had been. One owner paid to keep him, changed his mind, and handed the league's best roster its first-choice running back.

Everything else was minimum-priced volume: DK Metcalf aside, the remaining lots are a wall of single-bid, minimum-salary purchases. Cooper Kupp cost the minimum. So did Chase Young, Cameron Heyward and Alex Highsmith, and that is the defense.

Defensively he committed $17,000, or 10.9% of his money, ranking 7 of 12 by share. Against what he spent on two offensive players, that is the whole strategy in one line: buy the two best available skill players and fill seven defensive slots out of the bargain bin.

Contracts, Trades, Cuts and the Rookie Class

9 contract moves, 1 trade, 8 removals. The removals are where the cleverness lives.

On the day the auction roster locked he released George Pickens, one of the largest salaries on his books, and took no penalty at all — the entire amount came straight back to him. Tony Pollard went the same day at a smaller salary, this one carrying a penalty. Within a fortnight L.A. Looks had bought Pickens at auction for close to what C-Town had been paying him, and CBP had bought Pollard for slightly more than C-Town had been paying him.

He converted two players he had decided against into cap room, and two rival owners spent real auction budget acquiring them. That money became McCaffrey and Burrow. It is the most efficient sequence anybody ran this summer and it is worth being explicit that it is not luck: releasing before an auction rather than after it is what created the market.

The bill is not zero. $14,500 of penalty from those releases has no charge year stamped on it yet, so it sits in the ledger unassigned. Nothing has hit this season at $0, but that figure is real and it will land somewhere.

Sam LaPorta was extended in May and now sits on the bench as a Mid TE1 behind an Elite TE1, which is a lot of salary for a backup. James Cook was extended and restructured the same day, two operations that cut his current-year charge sharply. Burrow was signed to a long back-loaded deal. The rest are minimum-salary conversions of the defensive purchases.

The one trade is the three-way that shaped the top of the league. He sent Drake London and $19,000 of blind-bid money out and received Tetairoa McMillan. On the board he gave up materially more than he got back and paid cash on top of it — this is the leg of that deal that reads worst, and the other two franchises came away with the better of it. Three-way trades are rare in this league, and this is the only one ever completed through its own trade tool.

The rookie class barely exists: 2 picks, both defenders, both late, neither priceable by any source yet. He was not drafting this year.

The Verdict

He is 1 of 3 in his division, ahead of Cleon Ca$h and the Hawks, and he plays both twice. Cleon Ca$h is a genuine threat and the Hawks are last in the league — two games a year against the softest opponent available is a real structural advantage on top of everything else.

Is this the strongest team in the league? Yes. 1 of 12, 0 holes, 6 studs, the highest lineup value anybody has, and first at running back. Was it a good offseason? Also yes, and the two answers are connected rather than coincidental — he spent $156,000 and financed most of it by selling into a market he created himself.

The biggest advantage is the offense top to bottom, with an Elite WR1 on a minimum contract as the keystone. The biggest weakness is a defense in which five of seven starters grade replacement-level — a genuine vulnerability in a league where three other owners have built elite defensive fronts for very little money, and one he has $13,000 of room to address.
