Early Preseason

April was a work in progress. Blake had 18 active players and $134,000 in salary, and 2 top-twelve starters, Puka Nacua, an Elite WR1, and Omarion Hampton at running back. Brock Purdy and Quinshon Judkins were solid. The 5 holes were Terry McLaurin at the second receiver spot, Brenton Strange at tight end, Rico Dowdle and Chris Godwin in the flexes, and Cam Ward in the superflex.

The spring was about keeping his young receivers. Both of his expiring rookie deals were extended in May: Nacua for $22,000 a year for 2 years, a TCV of $44,000, and Jayden Reed for one more year at $15,000. In March he had already sent his 2027 first to Sex Manther for 1.10, before the trade bot graded deals; graded now on what was known that day, it comes out C+ for Blake. Jacoby Brissett was already on a $16,000 tag by April. No cuts.

Pre-Auction

Blake used 1.10 on Denzel Boston, one of 6 picks, and bought Emanuel Wilson at the Expired Rookie Auction for $2,000. In July he let the tag go, cutting Brissett and his $16,000 salary with no penalty, and sent a 2027 second to Sex Manther for George Kittle, with a little blind-bid money coming back the other way; the trade bot's second look graded it B- for him.

The big deal came in late July, and it was an extend-and-trade. Two months after extending Nacua, Blake sent him into a three-way trade with Real Deal Creel and C-Town and got back Drake London, Real Deal Creel's 2027 first, two depth players and $19,000 of blind-bid money, a B from the bot. Between the draft and the lock he took on $91,000 of salary in trades and sent out $22,000, and he walked into the auction with 23 active players and $184,000 in active-roster salary, more of both than in April.

The Auction and Preseason Contracts

Blake Bombers spent $129,000 at the Free Agent Auction, and 68.2% of it went on one player. Two restructures on the day of the three-way made the room: London, just acquired, went down to $20,000 this year, and McLaurin to $8,000 this year and $30,000 in 2027. Josh Allen then cost $88,000 after fifteen bids, the most anyone paid for anyone this summer; Blake beat the commissioner to him. DeVonta Smith cost $26,000, and the rest was defenders, a punter, Justice Hill at running back and depth.

What it bought: Allen, an Elite QB1 and the Elite buy; Smith, a High-end WR2 and the Good one; and Justice Hill, a minimum buy at running back who starts. The league table ranks the Bombers 5th of 12 for how much the auction improved the offense, and with Purdy in the superflex his quarterbacks rank 1st in the league.

After the auction came the second extend-and-trade. In August Reed, extended in May, went to CBP with three depth players and blind-bid money for Ricky Pearsall, Jonah Coleman and a 2027 fourth, another B, and the next day he moved some of London's money back into this season: $33,000 this year and $62,000 in 2027, up from $20,000 and down from $75,000. Smith signed for 3 years at $26,000 a year, a TCV of $78,000, one of 7 multi-year deals from the auction, and Will Anderson and AJ Barner were each extended one more year. He claimed 4 players off waivers, a kicker among them, and released the ones that did not stick at no cost.

The lineup it built has 4 top-twelve starters, Allen, Hampton, London and Kittle, with Judkins, Smith and Purdy solid, and 2 holes, both flexes: McLaurin and Hill. On MFL's projections the defense is even rather than strong in one place: the line, the linebackers and the secondary each rank 6th in the league, with Will Anderson a Low-end DL1 and Trey Hendrickson and Quay Walker a High-end DL2 and a High-end LB2. The punter, Ryan Rehkow, is a High-end PN1; the kicker is a waiver pickup and a footnote.

No starter is hurt. Pearsall came over from CBP already on injured reserve with a knee injury and is out for the season. Ty Simpson, on the taxi squad, is not hurt; he is listed out for the first two weeks on a coach's decision.

History and the Division

Blake has the best trophy case in the league. Since 2011 he has won 3 titles, in 2011, 2015 and 2023, more than any other owner, and finished runner-up in 2012 and 2014. He has made the playoffs 12 times in 15 seasons, the most in the league, and won 9 division titles, the last three of them in a row, 2023 through 2025. His career all-play record is 53.3%.

The new alignment put him in someone else's division. In the league's first Owner Divisional Draft, Derrick Whitman took Gride with the second pick and came back for Blake Bombers 7th overall; Blake had said in May that the draft would spice things up. Whitman calls LEGION OF BOOM his division because he put it together, but in July he picked Blake to win it, and the simulation agrees: Blake Bombers are the favorite, 52.5% to win it, with CBP at 35.9% and Gride at 11.6%, and 66.5% playoff odds and 10.2% title odds for the Bombers.

The division weeks are 1, 4, 8, 11 and 14, and none of them is a disaster. Week eight costs him Kittle, Purdy and Will Anderson; week eleven Judkins, London and two defenders, while CBP loses six starters that week; and week fourteen, the last of the regular season, has no byes anywhere in the division. Across the five weeks he loses 7 starters to byes, CBP 9 and Gride 7.

The next three years, filed live, as they happen. 2027: Allen. Division title. Playoffs. Sucks to be everyone else ha. 2028: title number four, which he will downplay in the same message he announces it. 2029: a model like this one ranks him third again, and he questions the model, ha.

Week one is a division week, and Blake asked on opening night for a season preview he was prepared to be torn apart by. He is not torn apart here. With 4 NFL teams done the Bombers already have 44.0 points, most of them from Purdy, who threw three touchdowns.
