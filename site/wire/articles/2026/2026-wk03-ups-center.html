UPS CENTER

At the desk: Stuart, Rich & Boomer · Kenny Mayne in the Coffee Shop

Bear Dunn swept the field; even Brock Purdy’s 43.3 could not lift Shawn Blake past him. Brian Cutting’s close loss shows why opportunity and points tell different stories. Stuart and Rich set the stage, Boomer takes the highlights, and Kenny Mayne reads the chat back to the people who wrote it.

1 Opening desk
Stuart

Week three put two very different scores on the same screen, Rich. Bear Dunn led the league with 254.8 and beat all three opponents. Brian Cutting scored 212.4, went 2–1, and lost the third game to Keith Creelman by only 6.5.

Rich

And look at how Cutting got there: Jahmyr Gibbs gave him 42.2, while Drake Maye scored 5.3 on 24.1 expected fantasy points. XFP estimates points from opportunity—passes, carries and targets. It is not the score that counted; that 18.8-point gap is larger than Cutting’s loss to Keith.

Stuart

Shawn Blake had the week’s best individual score from Brock Purdy—43.3—and still lost to Bear by 9.4. We’ll sort those performances from the results after Boomer runs the highlights. Boomer, take it away.

2 Boomer’s Three-Minute Rundown
Boomer

Thanks, Stu! Bear Dunn did not merely survive this week; he made everybody take a number. Matt Gerardi took the worst of it, Derrick Whitman could not catch him, and even Shawn Blake’s huge day fell short. Bear finished 3–0 on 254.8, and eleven-for-eleven against the full field in all-play.

Boomer

Shawn had Brock Purdy firing four touchdowns against Arizona for 43.3 UPS points, a full 26.5 above his last verified pre-kickoff projection. Shawn reached 245.4 himself, and Bear still beat him by 9.4. Some weeks you hit the ceiling and find somebody living upstairs.

Boomer

Here comes Jahmyr Gibbs for Brian Cutting, and do not blink. Cuts back left on the Jets—whoop!—and he could go all the way! Two rushing touchdowns, another through the air, 42.2 UPS points. Rumblin’, bumblin’, stumblin’ through traffic. Cutting took two games; Keith Creelman got the third.

Boomer

And that Keith–Cutting finish had a twist. Cutting’s offense and kicking units were better on the day. Keith’s defenders piled up 68.6 to Brian’s 45.3, enough to cover both gaps and leave Keith 6.5 ahead. The defense supplied the swing; those other points still counted.

Boomer

Ryan Bousquet scooped Darren Waller off the wire, started him, and got 13.2. Ryan squeezed past Brian Cross by 3.9, then lost to Cutting by 3.8 and Eric Martel by 16.2. Two games on a knife edge, one clear loss, and only one win to show for it.

Boomer

Derrick Whitman, here is the one that will sit on the bench in your memory. Kenyon Sadiq scored 29.8 while starter Kyle Pitts posted 2.0. Swap only those tight ends, hold every other score steady, and Derrick turns two losses into wins. That decision has a receipt.

Boomer

Do not leave Eric Martel out of the highlights. Juwan Johnson ran past his 10.7 pre-kickoff projection and scored 31.3 for Eric. Eric’s closest win, over Keith, was by only 5.9. That is the kind of surprise production that makes a close final feel very different.

Boomer

And Matt Gerardi could not find daylight. Three different opponents handed him the three largest defeats of the week: Bear, Chris Klingenberg, Eric Mannila. Three teams, one battered scoreboard. Stu, Rich, that is your division desk. I am out.

6 League landscape — current playoff and title odds
Stuart

The line that makes you look twice is Brian Cutting. He is 3–5, yet this forecast gives him a 92.7% playoff chance, nearly level with Bear Dunn at 93.4%. Cutting went 2–1 this week and scored 212.4; Bear went 3–0 and led the whole field at 254.8.

Rich

The model gives Cutting the strongest remaining legal lineup at 244.0 projected points a week. On September 29 he got Lamar Jackson, Andrew Van Ginkel and Tyler Huntley from Josh Martel; Baker Mayfield, Brian Thomas Jr. and Jonathan Allen went the other way. Picks moved too: Cutting sent his own 2027 first and second plus Matt Gerardi’s 2027 second, and got Josh’s 2027 fourth back.

Stuart

So we reran this same forecast, at the same cutoff, with only those six players put back on their old teams. With the trade, the model has Cutting at 92.7% to make the playoffs; without it, 82.8%. Call it eight to ten points, depending on whether the model re-tunes its uncertainty for the no-trade rosters. His title chance is 31.9% with the deal and 21.4% without it. That is a model comparison at one moment, not an odds move anybody watched happen, and the simulator gives those draft picks no value at all.

Rich

Eric’s sweep was not all cruise control. Keith pushed him to 224.8–218.9, then beat Cutting by 6.5 elsewhere. Both still have strong playoff chances: 88.5% for Eric, 83.5% for Keith. Those completed games are banked; we have not run the separate what-if tests needed to price either result on its own.

Stuart

Josh is the other side of it. Same rerun: 12.4% to make the playoffs with the deal, 26.0% with the six players back where they were—about eleven to fourteen points. His week was already rough—0–3 on 165.4. Brian Cross sits at 11.9% after scoring 204.7 and beating Josh. Trade test. A saved snapshot of the Week 4 model inputs (refreshed with the preview on Wed Sep 30) reproduces all 36 published playoff, division and title probabilities. Across six seeds and a 40,000-run check, Cutting’s lift stays between +7.8 and +10.2 and Josh’s drop between −11.1 and −13.6.
