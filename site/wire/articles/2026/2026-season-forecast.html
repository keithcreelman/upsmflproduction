The Board

C-Town Chivalry opens the season at number one. The Wire played 20,000 seasons from the rosters as they stand, and Bear Dunn's team came out with a projected all-play of 59.8%, 74.9% to make the playoffs and 20.8% to win the whole thing, the best odds in the league. Pure Greatness, the defending champion, is next at 14.8%, and Blake Bombers third at 10.2%.

Behind those three the table bunches. Five teams sit between 52.2% and 49.1% in projected all-play: HammerTime, Real Deal Creel, Sex Manther, CBP and Cleon Ca$h, which is close enough that a good month reorders them. The Long Haulers, the Hawks and L.A. Looks follow, and Gride is alone at the bottom at 38.3% with 20.9% playoff odds.

One word on how to read that column, because it is the easiest number in the paper to misread. Projected all-play is an AVERAGE over 20,000 seasons, and averages bunch: a team's good years and bad years cancel each other out, so nobody's average looks like a real season. Real seasons are far wider. Across the 16 completed seasons in the record, the team that led all-play won 73.3% of its matchups and the team that finished last in it won 22.9%; the simulation's own leader averages 75.9%. That is why the forecast table carries a typical season and the band that holds eight seasons in ten beside the average, and why the shape table below sets the simulated league against the real one, place by place.

The regular season is worth more than it looks, though less than the bracket makes it appear. In every one of the 16 completed seasons the team that led all-play also won its division, which means it took a first-round bye every time, and a bye means your season ends in the semi-final at worst. That floor is the bracket's doing, not the résumé's. What the résumé is worth is the top of it: the all-play leader has gone on to win the title 5 times, or 31.2% of seasons against the 8.3% an even split would give him, and 75.0% of champions came out of the top three in all-play. The simulation lands in the same place, at 77.1%.

The offseason that produced all this moved $1,352,000 across 196 auction lots and left $130,000 of cap room in the entire league against $3,470,000 committed. It did level the place out: league starting-lineup value rose from 283,202 in April to 380,665, holes in the twelve lineups fell from 51 to 29, and the gap between the best starting lineup and the worst closed from 4.74x to 2.48x. What it did not do is change the order much.

The two boards below rank every starting slot in the league, offense and defense kept apart so the comparison is even. C-Town tops both, which is the whole case for the top seed. The rest is less tidy. Cleon Ca$h starts one of the best offenses in the league and the simulation still has him 8th, because the defense and the kicker are near the bottom. The Long Haulers own the defensive line and the linebackers and sit 9th, because the receivers are thin. HammerTime starts the best receiver pair and the best tight end in the league alongside the worst quarterback room and the worst kicker. Gride is the only team with nowhere to hide: bottom three on both boards, and last at five of the seven defensive columns.

April: What Twelve Teams Had

In April half the league could not field a team. 6 of the 12 rosters had too few active players to fill the eighteen starting slots, which is less a scandal than a calendar: contracts run out, rookie deals expire, and nobody signs anybody until the auction. Where a roster could not field a lineup there was no lineup, so the April figures are the optimizer putting whoever was on hand into slots, never an owner's choice.

What the twelve had between them was 283,202 of starting-lineup value and 51 holes, and it was not spread evenly: the best starting lineup in the league was worth 4.74x the worst. Pure Greatness and C-Town arrived with something close to a team. Real Deal Creel had the smallest salary commitment in the league and a roster to match.

The one spring move that changed a contender was HammerTime's. In April Eric Martel sent Jalen Hurts and blind-bid money to The Long Haulers for two 2027 picks, then spent his own first-round pick on Kenneth Walker and his second on David Montgomery, and restructured Ja'Marr Chase and Walker to pay for it. That is the trade the quarterback column on the offense board still remembers.

Otherwise the league sat on its hands. Not one waiver claim or free-agent signing was made anywhere before the auction closed: all 42 of the league's pickups came afterwards.

The Draft and the Tags

Late May did two things at once. The rookie draft put 72 picks into the league, and the owners drafted their own divisions for the first time.

Four captains, seated by career all-play, took turns in a two-round snake at Brian Cross's house on draft night. Eric Martel's HammerTime sat 1st, Derrick Whitman's CBP 2nd, Bear Dunn's C-Town 3rd and Josh Martel's Sex Manther 4th. Martel opened by taking The Long Haulers 1st overall; Whitman took Gride 2nd; Dunn took the Hawks 3rd, exactly as he had said he would in January; and Josh Martel took Real Deal Creel 4th and doubled back for L.A. Looks 5th, rivalry picks both.

The room's rule of thumb, as the league bot put it the next morning: going first meant your captain thought you were the weakest team in the draft. By that logic the captains were broadly right, and the last team on the board was the defending champion, Pure Greatness, which landed with HammerTime 8th and gave Outlast PG Time the last two champions in one division.

The tags told their own story. They went out in May and most of them were chips rather than keeps: Amon-Ra St. Brown was tagged and traded, George Pickens was tagged and released on lock day, and Christian McCaffrey was tagged, handed back in July and bought at auction by somebody else. The ones that stuck were cheap or quiet: Kamren Curl, Myles Garrett, Javonte Williams and Trevor Lawrence.

The Two Auctions

The Free Agent Auction is where the league actually built its teams. Across both auctions the twelve spent $1,352,000 on 196 players, $1,315,000 of it at the Free Agent Auction itself, and the top price of the summer was $88,000, which Blake Bombers paid for Josh Allen after fifteen bids.

Almost all of the season's roster-building happened in those thirteen days. League starting-lineup value went into the lock at 295,942 and came out of the close at 382,118 -- nearly the whole of the year's improvement, in under a fortnight.

The phase table below is the league's offseason in one block: what each lineup was worth in April, at the lock, at the close and now. Read it and the pattern is not that the rich got richer. The biggest gains went to the teams that arrived with the least -- CBP and Real Deal Creel, who had the thinnest April rosters in the league, took two of the three largest -- while the two best April rosters, Pure Greatness and Cleon Ca$h, spent modestly and moved barely at all. That is the whole reason the gap between the best lineup and the worst closed from 4.74x to 2.48x while the order at the top stayed put.

Defense was where the money was not. Only The Long Haulers and HammerTime put a serious share of their auction money into defenders, and the defense board says it worked: The Long Haulers start the best defensive line and the best linebackers in the league. Everybody else bought offense, and the two boards below the forecast show who got what for it.

Since the Auction

Since the auction closed the board has barely moved. League starting-lineup value was 382,118 at the close and is 380,665 now, and most of that difference is one suspension.

That is not the same as nothing happening. The league made all 42 of its waiver and free-agent pickups in this stretch, Real Deal Creel more than anyone, and three trades went through after the close. What there is not, anywhere, is money: $3,470,000 is committed and $130,000 of room is left across all 12 teams, so the owners with the biggest holes are mostly not the owners who can fix them.

The forecast holds 7 starters out of week one, and it holds out two more by hand: Sam Darnold for week two and A.J. Brown for weeks two through four, both hurt in the opener and neither listed by MFL yet.

Week one itself is an opening data point and nothing else. Only a handful of NFL teams had finished when this was written, and the teams whose players happened to play look better than the teams whose players did not. It is not evidence, and the paper will not treat it as any.

The Divisions, Strongest to Weakest

The divisions are new this year, the owners drafted them themselves, and they hold until 2028 -- which is the only reason it is worth arguing about which one is the strongest. On the simulation's own currency, it is Outlast PG Time: 52.5% mean projected all-play across Pure Greatness, HammerTime and The Long Haulers, 1.63 of the six playoff places, and the best defense in the league. It also spent the least of any division at the auction.

DOG POUND 4 LIFE is a hair behind at 51.6% and holds the most title equity in the league at 30.8% -- but two thirds of that is C-Town alone, with Cleon Ca$h and the Hawks behind him. It is the division with the best team and not the best three.

Maddie Mondays and LEGION OF BOOM finish level, at 48.1% and 47.8%, and by projected points a week they are inside a point of each other -- call it a tie and mean it. They are level in completely different ways. Maddie Mondays is the closest division in the league, Real Deal Creel, Sex Manther and L.A. Looks packed together, and it spent more at the auction than anyone. LEGION OF BOOM is the widest: Blake Bombers third in the league, Gride twelfth, and CBP in between. That is a floor problem, not a ceiling problem.

Which leaves the thing the whole paper has to say out loud. This is a projection, not a promise. Tested on the last five seasons it explained about two fifths of the spread in final all-play and ranked the field at only a third to two fifths in each of the last three, so read every number here as odds. Gride, last in the table, still finishes top three in all-play in a meaningful share of the 20,000 runs. C-Town, first in it, finishes last in a few of them too.
