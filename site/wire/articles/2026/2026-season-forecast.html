The Forecast

Bear Dunn asked for a preseason forecast in August, and Shawn Blake reminded the room on opening night that he was still waiting for one. Here it is. The Wire played the season 20,000 times from every roster as it stands, on MFL's own weekly projections, with each team's edge trimmed back by nearly a third, because projections always think they know more than they do. Players MFL lists as out sit until it expects them back, and so do two players hurt in the opener whom MFL has not listed yet: Sam Darnold for week two and A.J. Brown for weeks two through four.

C-Town Chivalry is the favorite: first in projected all-play at 59.8%, a 74.9% chance at the playoffs and 20.8% title odds, the best in the league. Pure Greatness, the defending champion, is right behind at 14.8%, and Blake Bombers are third at 10.2%.

Behind the top three it gets crowded. HammerTime, Real Deal Creel, Sex Manther and CBP are fourth through seventh, less than a game apart in projected wins and all of them between 52.0% and 54.9% to make the playoffs. Derrick Whitman, whose CBP is 7th, said in July that he likes being the underdog. Cleon Ca$h, The Long Haulers and the Hawks follow, and L.A. Looks and Gride bring up the rear, Gride with 20.9% playoff odds: a long shot, not a closed door.

By division, DOG POUND 4 LIFE is C-Town's to lose, a 57.2% chance, and LEGION OF BOOM leans toward Blake Bombers at 52.2%. Eric Martel called HammerTime against Pure Greatness a coin flip in July and gave himself the edge; the forecast is less generous to him, with Pure Greatness at 49.5% and HammerTime at 30.6%. The real coin flip is Maddie Mondays: Real Deal Creel 40.5%, Sex Manther 38.3% and L.A. Looks 21.2%.

A forecast is not a promise. Tested on the last five seasons, this one got the order roughly right in 2021 and 2022 and much less right since, so read every number here as odds, not a verdict.

The Divisional Draft

This is year one of a three-year alignment, and the first the owners chose themselves. The rule passed in May: the four owners with the best career all-play records became captains and drafted their divisions in a two-round snake on the night of the rookie draft, most of the league in Brian Cross's house and the rest dialed in.

The captains, in seat order, were Eric Martel of HammerTime, Derrick Whitman of CBP, Bear Dunn of C-Town and Josh Martel of Sex Manther. The room's rule of thumb, as the bot's recap put it the next morning: going first meant your captain thought you were the weakest team. So when Eric Martel opened the draft by taking The Long Haulers, the room knew what it meant, and Cross took it in stride: when Ryan Bousquet said it was going to be a long haul, Cross allowed that at least he had picked the right team name.

With the second pick, Derrick Whitman took Gride. Dunn took the Hawks third, which he had announced in January, the day the captain draft was first floated. Josh Martel, who had said captains would pick for rivalry as much as weakness, took Real Deal Creel fourth and doubled back for L.A. Looks fifth. Dunn added Cleon Ca$h, Whitman took Blake Bombers, and the last team on the board was the defending champion, Pure Greatness, which landed with HammerTime. Eric Martel had predicted in May that the last team left would be a strong one. He was right, and now he has to play it.

Cross, the first team picked, has made the joke his own. A month later he pitched names for his new division, and the best of them, in the box here, casts The Long Haulers as the problem sitting next to two recent champions. It is simply accurate: HammerTime won the title in 2024 and Pure Greatness in 2025. The forecast has The Long Haulers 9th.

The Lay of the Land

The offseason in the chat started where the last season ended, with Brian Cutting explaining how he won his first title: he traded Jordan Love for Drake Maye and a first-round pick. Ryan Bousquet named the Free Agent Auction while it was still running, and it lived up to the name. Blake Bombers beat the commissioner to Josh Allen, and Real Deal Creel spent the most anyway.

Shawn Blake has since become the league's injury desk. Hours before the opener he broke the news of Brock Bowers's knee surgery, bad news for C-Town, the forecast's favorite, and the room treated it with the gravity you would expect. Blake then spent Thursday night's game defending George Kittle, the tight end the trade bot had mocked for his age and his Achilles, and Brian Cutting answered for everyone.

Week one is a division week for everyone. In Maddie Mondays, Real Deal Creel put in a round of waiver claims, and Josh Martel was not impressed. The last word goes to Brian Cross, who hosted the draft night and still loves the league.
