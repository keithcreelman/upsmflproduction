The Lineup

Drake Maye grades Elite QB1 on a rookie contract. Jahmyr Gibbs is an Elite RB1. Justin Jefferson is a High-end WR1 with Chris Olave at High-end WR2 and Zay Flowers, another High-end WR2, in the flex. The receiver room ranks 1 in the league, which is first, and the backfield ranks 3 on Gibbs and Ashton Jeanty at Low-end RB1.

Quarterback ranks 4 with Baker Mayfield at Mid QB2 behind Maye, which is the only place the offense is merely good rather than excellent. Davante Adams fills the second flex at Low-end WR2. There is not a single slot on this offense occupied by somebody the board refuses to rank.

The defense has two genuinely elite pieces and a soft middle. Myles Garrett grades Elite DL1 and Tykee Smith Elite DB1 — two of the best defenders in the league on one roster. Behind them Nik Bonitto is a Mid DL1 and Derwin James a Mid DB3, and then it thins out badly: Christian Rozeboom and Nakobe Dean are both Replacement-level LB, and Nick Bosa fills the last flex as a Replacement-level DL, which is a sentence that should not be possible and is.

The one real soft spot on offense is tight end, ranked 11, where Mark Andrews grades Replacement-level TE. That is the whole list of complaints.

Composite 2 of 12, 4 studs, 0 holes. Nobody else in the league has zero. This is not a team of a few superstars papering over gaps, nor an adequate-everywhere roster — it is genuinely both, top-heavy and complete at the same time, which is the hardest combination to build.

The Bridge

Here is the whole offseason in one number. In April he had 15 active players. At the auction roster lock, three months later, he had 15. The same number. Legal-lineup value over that span moved from 42,534 to 42,534 — that is to say, not at all. Studs stayed at 4, holes stayed at 0.

He could not legally field a lineup at either stage, and it did not matter, because the reason was roster size rather than roster quality. He was carrying $236,000 against 15 bodies, which is the most concentrated payroll in the league by a distance. Everyone else spent the summer replacing expired contracts. He had kept his.

The auction filled the roster out to 34 active and lifted lineup value to 42,796, a change of almost nothing. One priced slot changed occupant all year: Mark Andrews replaced Dalton Schultz at tight end, and both of them grade Replacement-level TE, so even that was lateral.

There is a version of this article that calls that passivity. It is not. When every priced slot already holds a player the board ranks, activity is a cost rather than a virtue, and the discipline to sit still while eleven other owners shovelled money at their own rosters is the single most impressive thing anybody did this summer.

The Auctions

Nothing at the Expired Rookie Auction: 0 lots, $0. He had no expiring holes to fill from a pool of players whose rookie deals ran out without a second one.

At the Free Agent Auction he bought more players than almost anybody — 19 lots — for $53,000, which is a fraction of what the top spenders laid out. That is the trick: volume at the bottom of the market rather than a headline signing.

The bargains are absurd. Tyreek Hill cost less than some teams pay a punter. Mark Andrews, Derwin James and Nik Bonitto all came in single-digit thousands. Most of those lots went uncontested at a single bid.

His largest purchase was Aaron Rodgers at $14,000, 26.4% of his auction money, and Rodgers grades Replacement-level QB and sits on the bench. That is the one buy that did not work, and the reason it barely matters is that it cost less than a sixth of what the league's most expensive lot went for at $88,000. Being wrong cheaply is a skill.

Defensively he put $13,000 in, which is 24.5% of his money and ranks 10 of 12 by share — a real commitment, in proportion, on a small budget. It is consistent with his record: 244 defensive acquisitions ranks 3, and points per started defender ranks 2, second in the league.

Contracts, Trades, Cuts and the Rookie Class

7 contract moves, 4 trades, 6 removals, $0 of penalty charged. Clean, and quietly the most strategically coherent sheet in the league.

Myles Garrett was tagged. Read the price on that row and then read his grade again: an Elite DL1, the best defensive player in the league, on a tag that costs less than what several owners in this league are paying a backup quarterback. That is the best value on any roster in this competition.

Justin Jefferson was moved onto a multi-year deal, and Tyreek Hill, Derwin James, Mark Andrews and Chuba Hubbard were all converted off auction wins. None of them is at the league minimum, whatever the small figures suggest.

The trades are where the plan shows. All four are pick work and every one of them sends 2026 out and brings 2027 back. Quentin Johnston went to The Long Haulers for a 2027 third. A 2026 second went to Real Deal Creel for a lower 2026 second and a 2027 third. That lower second and a 2026 fourth went to Gride for a 2027 second. A 2026 second went to HammerTime for a 2026 fourth and another 2027 third. He came out holding four extra picks next year and made only 5 selections this year, none of them before 4.01.

He sold a draft he did not need to buy a draft he might. With a roster this complete and this expensive, the only real threat is age and cap, and picks are the currency for both. It is the clearest single strategy any owner executed this summer.

The removals are housekeeping: a taxi player whose salary was never on the cap, Zach Ertz, and 4 bodies signed and discarded inside the same offseason. Nothing charged.

The rookie class is thin by design. Max Klare at 4.01 and Elijah Sarratt at 4.06 sit in bands where history says almost nothing usable emerges, and Josiah Trotter at 6.04 is a defender no source can price yet. He was not trying to win this draft.

The Verdict

He is 1 of 3 in his division, ahead of The Long Haulers and HammerTime, and he plays both twice. That is the most comfortable divisional position in the league — the nearest team to him is four places back overall.

Is this the strongest team in the league? Very nearly: 2 of 12, and it is first at receiver with an Elite QB1, an Elite RB1, an Elite DL1 and an Elite DB1 on the same sheet. Was it a good offseason? That question almost does not apply. He did the least of anybody, spent $53,000 in total, and finished the summer exactly as strong as he started it — which, from 0 holes, is the correct outcome.

The biggest advantage is the top of the roster: Maye, Gibbs, Jefferson, Garrett, Tykee Smith. The biggest weakness is linebacker, where Rozeboom and Dean are both replacement-level and Nick Bosa is filling a flex he cannot currently justify, with tight end close behind at 11 in the league.

One caveat on the crown jewel, and it is one game. 2 NFL teams had played when this was written. Drake Maye, the Elite QB1 on the rookie deal, threw three interceptions and banked 13.6 points anyway, most of it on the ground. The board's opinion of him and the way he actually played that night are not the same opinion. It is one night, and nobody has designated him anything — but it is the first live evidence, and it argues for the rushing floor rather than the arm.
