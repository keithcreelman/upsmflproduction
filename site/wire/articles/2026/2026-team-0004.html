The Champion Spent the Summer Standing Still, and It Was the Right Call

Brian Cutting won the 2025 title, kept every starter who won it, and spent just $38,000 on offense at the Free Agent Auction. The preseason simulation ranks Pure Greatness 2nd, favourite in its division.

Early Preseason

The champion's April roster looked like a champion's. Cutting carried 15 active players and $236,000 in salary, and 5 top-twelve starters: Drake Maye, an Elite QB1, Jahmyr Gibbs, an Elite RB1, Ashton Jeanty beside him, and Justin Jefferson and Chris Olave at receiver. Zay Flowers, Davante Adams and Baker Mayfield were solid in the flexes and the superflex. The one hole was tight end, where Dalton Schultz grades replacement-level.

He barely touched it before the draft. One trade: Quentin Johnston and his $18,000 salary went to the Long Haulers in May for a 2027 third, which also settled Johnston's expiring rookie deal. The other three rookie deals that ran out, Darnell Washington, Quan Martin and Stetson Bennett, were let go. No cuts, no extensions.

Pre-Auction

The tag was the one expensive-looking decision, and it was not expensive: Cutting tagged Myles Garrett in May at $10,000, and Garrett is an Elite DL1. In June Justin Jefferson got a new contract, $71,000 this year and $96,000 in all, the deal that keeps the league's top receiver room together.

Draft day was about next year. Cutting traded out of the second round three times, sending 2.06, 2.09, 2.12 and 4.12 away for 4.01 and three 2027 picks, a second and two thirds. Add the third he got for Johnston and he goes into next spring with extra picks in both of those rounds. This year he made 5 picks from 4.01 down, Max Klare, Elijah Sarratt, Caleb Douglas, Cole Payton and Josiah Trotter, a class that will not touch this lineup. The only releases before the lock were Nic Scourton off the taxi squad and Zach Ertz, both at no cost.

The Auction and Preseason Contracts

The auction was maintenance. Cutting won 19 players for $53,000, and the most expensive was Aaron Rodgers at $14,000, who is on the bench. Mark Andrews cost $7,000 to take over at tight end, and James Conner and Chuba Hubbard $5,000 each for running back depth.

On the league table that money finishes near the bottom, 11th of 12 for what each dollar added, and the What it bought column says why: depth, all of it. That is not a failed auction. There was almost nothing to add to an offense that went in with no hole but tight end, and the owners who bought starters were buying what Cutting already had.

The contract work was light. 4 auction buys got multi-year contracts, Andrews at $7,000 this year and $14,000 in all among them. There were no restructures, no waiver claims, and the four players released after the auction all went at no cost.

So the lineup it built is the lineup he started with. 5 top-twelve starters, Maye, Gibbs, Jeanty, Jefferson and Olave; receivers that rank 1st in the league and backs that rank 2nd; and 1 hole, still tight end, now Andrews instead of Schultz. The kicker, Harrison Mevis, is MFL's top-projected at the position, and the defense, led by Garrett, is a footnote that happens to be a good one.

Historical Context

Cutting has been here from the start, 2010, 16 completed seasons. The title everyone remembers is the most recent one, 2025, the same year he had the league's best all-play record, and it came three years after a runner-up finish in 2022. The rest of the record is steadier than it is spectacular: 9 playoff trips, 9 division titles, and a 51.5% career all-play record.

Outlast PG Time holds the last two champions, and the simulation still makes Cutting the favourite in it: 2nd in the league, a 48.9% chance at the division, 70.1% playoff odds and 14.7% title odds. HammerTime, the 2024 champion, is 4th, and the Long Haulers 9th. He plays each of them five times.
