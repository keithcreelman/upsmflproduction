Pure Greatness Spent the Offseason Changing Almost Nothing, Which Was the Correct Answer

Brian Cutting never had a hole in a priced slot: not in April, not entering the auction, not when it closed. He ranks 2nd of 12, and the most expensive player he bought all summer is on his bench.

The Lineup

The best way to judge Brian Cutting's summer is to notice how little he had to do. Drake Maye is an Elite QB1, Jahmyr Gibbs is an Elite RB1, and Justin Jefferson leads a receiver room, Chris Olave and Zay Flowers right behind him, that ranks 1st in the league. While everyone else spent the offseason hunting for starters, he spent it admiring his. What follows is the case that standing still was the smartest move anybody made, and the one spot on the field where it could still cost him.

Davante Adams fills the second flex at Low-end WR2. Quarterback ranks 5th with Baker Mayfield at Mid QB2 behind Maye, good rather than excellent, and the one real soft spot is tight end, ranked 11th, where Mark Andrews grades Replacement-level TE.

On defense the thin spots are linebacker, where Christian Rozeboom and Nakobe Dean both grade Replacement-level LB, and the last flex, where Nick Bosa is a Replacement-level DL.

Composite 2nd of 12, 4 studs, 0 holes. Only C-Town Chivalry, the one team ranked above him, also has no holes today, and it carries more studs; but C-Town began the year with a hole, and he did not. It is neither a few stars papering over gaps nor a roster that is merely adequate everywhere.

The Bridge

The whole offseason fits in a sentence: almost nothing moved. In April he had 15 active players, 4 studs and no hole in a priced slot. At the auction roster lock, three months later, he still had 15 active players, the same studs and the same nine best players at the priced positions.

He was carrying $236,000 against those 15 players in April, the most concentrated payroll in the league, and $239,000 at the lock. Every other team opened April with at least one hole in a priced slot; the nine players filling his were all still under contract.

The auction filled the roster out to 34 active players and changed exactly one priced slot: Mark Andrews replaced Dalton Schultz at tight end, and both grade Replacement-level TE, so even that was lateral. His starting offense came out of the auction barely better than it went in.

When no priced slot has a hole in it, there is little an auction can add, and with $239,000 already committed at the lock, there was not much room to add it with.

The Auctions

Nothing at the Expired Rookie Auction: 0 players won, $0 spent. He had no expiring holes to fill from a pool of players whose rookie deals ran out without a second one.

At the Free Agent Auction he bought as many players as anybody — 19, tied for the most in the league — for $53,000, which is a fraction of what the top spenders laid out. That was the approach: volume at the bottom of the market, and 4 of those buys were gone again before the season started.

On offense that money bought almost nothing, because there was almost nothing left to buy. He spent $38,000 on quarterbacks, backs, receivers and tight ends at the Free Agent Auction, and for what it bought he ranks 11th of 12, ahead of only Cleon Ca$h. He walked in with the most valuable starting offense in the league and no holes in it, and Cleon, the one owner behind him, walked in with the second-most valuable. Blake Bombers, by contrast, got more for every dollar than anyone.

The prices were low: every buy but one came in at single-digit thousands, and most went uncontested, at a single bid.

His largest purchase was Aaron Rodgers at $14,000, 26.4% of his auction money, and Rodgers grades Replacement-level QB and sits on the bench. That was his one big buy, and it did not work, but it barely matters: it cost less than a sixth of what the league's most expensive player went for at $88,000. Being wrong cheaply is a skill.

Defenders took $13,000, 24.5% of his money and the third-largest share in the league, a heavy tilt toward the one position group the waiver wire restocks all season, for a defense that already ranked 2nd in points per started defender over the last three seasons.

Contracts, Trades, Cuts and the Rookie Class

7 contract moves, 4 trades, 6 removals, $0 of penalty charged. Clean, and strategically coherent.

Myles Garrett was tagged, for less than the $14,000 he is paying Aaron Rodgers to sit on his own bench.

Justin Jefferson was moved onto a multi-year deal, and Tyreek Hill, Derwin James, Mark Andrews and Chuba Hubbard were all converted off auction wins. None of them is at the league minimum.

The trades are where the plan shows. Every one of them brings 2027 back: three are pure pick work, and the first sent a player out. Quentin Johnston went to The Long Haulers for a 2027 third. A 2026 second went to Real Deal Creel for a lower 2026 second and a 2027 third. That lower second and a 2026 fourth went to Gride for a 2027 second. A 2026 second went to HammerTime for a 2026 fourth and another 2027 third. He came out holding four extra picks next year and made only 5 selections this year, none of them before 4.01.

He sold a draft he did not need to buy a draft he might.

The removals are housekeeping: a taxi player whose salary was never on the cap, Zach Ertz, and 4 bodies signed and discarded inside the same offseason. Nothing charged.

The rookie class is thin by design. Max Klare at 4.01 and Elijah Sarratt at 4.06 sit in bands where history says almost nothing usable emerges, and Josiah Trotter at 6.04 is a defensive rookie who has not played an NFL season yet. He was not trying to win this draft.

The Verdict

He is 1st of 3 in his division, ahead of The Long Haulers and HammerTime, and he plays each of them five times. That is one of the most comfortable divisional positions in the league — the nearest team to him is four places back overall.

Is this the strongest team in the league? Very nearly: 2nd of 12, and it is first at receiver with an Elite QB1 and an Elite RB1 on the same sheet. Was it a good offseason? That question almost does not apply. He spent $53,000, less than all but two owners, because there was no hole to fill and little cap room to fill one with, and he finished the summer about as strong as he started it, which, from 0 holes, is the correct outcome.

The biggest advantage is an offense with stars at the top and no hole anywhere below them: Maye, Gibbs, Jefferson. The biggest weakness is tight end, 11th in the league, where Mark Andrews grades Replacement-level TE, no better than the Dalton Schultz he replaced; the thin middle of the defense is the cheapest problem in the league to fix.

One caveat on the crown jewel, and it is one game. 2 NFL teams had played when this was written. Drake Maye, the Elite QB1, threw three interceptions and still banked 13.6 points, his legs making up some of what the picks cost him. It is one night, it grades nothing, and it is no reason yet to doubt him.
