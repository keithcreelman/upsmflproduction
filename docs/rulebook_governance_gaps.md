# Governance gaps — proposals for the commissioner

**Status: PROPOSALS. None of this is a rule.** Nothing here is in `docs/league_context_v1.md`,
nothing here has been voted on, and nothing here appears in the member-facing rulebook.
It is drafted so it can go into the Discord rule-proposal pipeline with the commissioner's
name on it, one at a time.

Written 2026-08-15 from a review of public contract/dynasty league constitutions
(Dynasty Fantasy League, Tennessee Dynasty, Eh Hole Club, Cheat Sheet War Room, FFPC,
Masters Dynasty, Elon FF, plus the FF Unlimited 18-section template).

**The finding:** the UPS rules cover the *game* more completely than any league reviewed,
including Dynasty Fantasy League, which is the closest structural twin. What is missing is
the *club* — the six sections that decide whether year 15 happens.

---

## 0. Do these two first — they are live problems, not future ones

### 0.1 Stat corrections have no written policy

MyFantasyLeague does **not** apply NFL stat corrections automatically. Corrections come from
Elias, land midweek, and sit under *For Commissioners → Statistic Changes* for the commissioner
to apply or decline. Yahoo and ESPN auto-apply. MFL does not.

So every week a discretionary human decision can change a completed game result, and nothing
written says when it does or stops doing so. In a playoff week this is the argument that ends
a league.

This needs a policy regardless of whether anything else here is adopted.

### 0.2 There is no written vote threshold

The Hall bot auto-closes votes on a threshold that appears nowhere in canon — no "51%",
no "75%", no "90%". The three May-2026 rules passed 7-0-0, which is consistent with a simple
majority, but it was never defined. Today every rule change is legitimate only because nobody
has objected. The first contested vote is the one that breaks.

Legacy rulebooks did define it — the 2018 book had 51% offseason / 75% for dues, the 2014
bylaws had 51% offseason / 90% in-season — but none of that survived into canon.

## 1. Changing the Rules

**Why:** see 0.2. Highest priority in this document.

**Field practice:** Tennessee Dynasty uses two-thirds with non-votes counted as abstentions.
Elon FF requires unanimity — do not copy this; in a 14-year league one grudge freezes the
rulebook permanently. Cheat Sheet War Room uses plurality with a commissioner tiebreak — too
loose for cap mechanics.

**Two mechanics worth taking outright.** Dynasty Fantasy League: changes do not take effect
until the following calendar year unless consent is unanimous. That single clause stops
mid-season rule-shopping by whoever the rule currently hurts. And Tennessee lets the
commissioner make wording fixes that do not change interpretation without a vote — necessary
when the document is 14 years old.

> ### Changing the Rules
>
> Any owner can propose a rule change. Post it in Discord as a proposal, written in the exact
> words you want voted on.
>
> Proposals open the day after the championship game and close March 1. The commissioner runs
> each vote through the bot for three days and announces the closing time when the vote opens.
>
> A proposal passes with 8 of 12 votes. Not voting counts as abstaining, and abstaining does
> not lower the number needed to pass.
>
> Changes take effect at the start of the next league year. A change takes effect sooner only
> if all 12 owners agree.
>
> Three things need all 12 votes: changing the number of teams, ending the salary cap or
> contracts, and changing how rules get changed.
>
> A proposal made during the season waits for the next window.
>
> The commissioner can fix typos, broken numbers, and wording that does not change what a rule
> does. Everything else needs a vote.

**Commissioner decisions needed:** the threshold (8 of 12 proposed), the proposal window
(current draft uses the offseason, which may not fit the May/July calendar), and whether the
supermajority list is right.

## 2. Playing to Win

**Why:** UPS awards pick 1.01 to the winner of the consolation bracket. That is a deliberate
design — Cheat Sheet War Room has run it 14 seasons precisely because winning the loser bracket
means you cannot tank your way into it. But it only removes the incentive *inside* the bracket.
A team on the playoff bubble is still better off missing the playoffs and entering a bracket it
can win. The clause that used to counterbalance this was dropped when canon was rebuilt.

**The structural fix is one sentence and is worth more than any conduct rule:**

> The consolation bracket is seeded by regular-season record. The best team that misses the
> playoffs is the 1 seed.

Losing more games now buys a worse path to 1.01, not a better one.

**Then the conduct floor, in two layers.** Dynasty Fantasy League protects rebuilding
explicitly while requiring a complete legal lineup every week. FFPC enumerates the non-lineup
vectors — skipping waivers, not using IR, rostering deliberately weak players — which matters
here because a cap league with a taxi squad has more tanking levers than lineup alone.

> ### Playing to Win
>
> You can rebuild. You cannot tank.
>
> Rebuilding is trading veterans for picks, letting contracts run out, and taking cap hits now
> to have room later. That is the league working as designed.
>
> Tanking is trying to lose. Every week you start a full, legal lineup of players expected to
> play. Empty starting slots, players on bye, and players listed Out or on IR are not a lineup.
>
> If your lineup is short or illegal at kickoff, the commissioner fills it with the
> highest-projected eligible player on your bench. The first time in a season is a warning.
>
> Lineups are not the only way to tank. Cutting productive players for nothing, sitting out
> waivers all season, and rostering players you know are worse to hold your score down all count.
>
> If you think an owner is tanking, say so in Discord and show your reasons. The commissioner
> rules within 72 hours. Penalties go from a warning, to the commissioner setting that team's
> lineup for the rest of the season, to losing a first-round rookie pick, to removal.

**Commissioner decisions needed:** the penalty ladder (the draft above deliberately omits the
cash fine and pick forfeiture the source league used — decide whether you want teeth), and
whether consolation reseeding needs its own vote since it changes a payout-adjacent bracket.

## 3. The Commissioner

**Why:** fourteen years and a strong commissioner is a strength. The problem is that none of it
is written, so it cannot be pointed at during an argument and it does not survive a handover.
Canon leans on commissioner discretion in at least eight places — the Jail Bird rule, the
fourth-offense league-fit review, the new-owner window, the Round 6 pick reversal — without
ever establishing the authority.

The 2024 rulebook had it and it was dropped: *"The commissioner has full authority on decisions
that fall outside the rulebook. In cases where league precedent has been set, this will guide
the decision-making process."*

No league reviewed had a succession plan. For a 14-year league with one strong commissioner,
that is the largest unwritten single point of failure.

> ### The Commissioner
>
> The commissioner runs the league year. He sets the calendar, runs the auction and the rookie
> draft, processes transactions, keeps the cap and contract records, collects dues, and pays out.
>
> The commissioner decides anything the rules do not cover. That decision holds for the rest of
> the season. If the same thing comes up again, it goes to a vote as a rule change.
>
> The commissioner can fix a clear mistake — a mis-entered salary, a missed waiver, a
> transaction the site processed wrong — as long as no games have been played since.
>
> The commissioner cannot change a rule on his own, cannot change your cap or a contract without
> telling you, and cannot rule on anything involving his own team.
>
> When the commissioner's team is involved, the two longest-tenured owners who are not involved
> decide instead.
>
> If the commissioner steps down or goes quiet for two weeks during the season, the
> longest-tenured owner runs the league until the league picks a replacement.

**Commissioner decision needed:** whether to include a removal clause. The source leagues all
have one; it is the commissioner's call whether that belongs in a league he founded.

## 4. Settling Disputes

**Why:** there is no written process for two owners disagreeing about what a rule means.

**Avoid** the model with a week-long discussion period and an outside "fantasy expert" tiebreak.
A week-long argument mid-season is worse than any ruling. League-wide votes on two-owner
disputes divide the league.

**Use** a small panel with a hard clock.

> ### Settling Disputes
>
> If two owners read a rule differently, either one posts the question in Discord. State the
> rule and both readings. Do not settle it in DMs.
>
> The commissioner answers within 48 hours, in writing. During the season, before the next
> kickoff.
>
> If the commissioner's team is affected, the two longest-tenured owners not involved answer.
>
> Either owner can appeal once, within 24 hours. Three owners hear it — the commissioner and
> one owner chosen by each side — and decide within 72 hours by majority. That decision is final.
>
> Rulings get written down and kept with the rules. The same question gets the same answer
> next year.

**Note:** the last line already half-exists in the codebase — `hall_qa_log` stores
`keith_ruling` rows marked AUTHORITATIVE. This proposal would make the written-down part a rule
rather than a bot implementation detail.

## 5. Joining and Leaving

**Why:** UPS has the asset half — dispersal draft, new-owner cap relief, penalty wipe — and none
of the people half. Canon has no admission process, no probation, no removal, no orphan-team
handling. The 2024 rulebook had an admission quiz and a two-season probation; both were dropped.

**Best practice found:** vote on a candidate *before* anyone is promised a spot. That avoids the
conversation where a friend of a friend already thinks he is in.

> ### Joining the League
>
> The league keeps a waiting list. Any owner can add a name. Add the name before you tell them
> anything.
>
> When a team opens up, the commissioner posts the candidates and runs a vote. Vote first, offer
> the team second.
>
> A new owner commits to two seasons.
>
> ### Teams Without Owners
>
> A team is orphaned when the owner quits, goes quiet for two weeks during the season, or misses
> the dues deadline.
>
> The commissioner runs an orphaned team until it has an owner. He starts the highest-projected
> legal lineup each week and makes no trades. An orphaned team cannot win a payout.
>
> ### Removing an Owner
>
> An owner can be removed by a vote for any of these: not setting lineups, not answering league
> communication for two weeks in season, not paying dues, tanking, or colluding.
>
> Removal waits for the offseason unless the owner is doing damage right now. Raise it in
> Discord, let the owner answer, then vote.

**Skip:** offering an orphaned team to existing owners first. It lets an owner strip their roster
and jump to a better one.

## 6. Collusion

**Why:** a salary cap plus real contracts creates collusion vectors no redraft league has, and
almost none of the reviewed constitutions address them, because almost none of those leagues have
contracts. Canon mentions collusion only as a trade-veto trigger, and contradicts itself on
whether vetoes exist at all.

The two vectors specific to UPS that no reviewed league names: **salary dumps to a team with
nothing to play for**, and **cut-and-claim arrangements**, which blind-bid waivers make easy.

> ### Collusion
>
> Owners do not vote on trades. A trade is not collusion because it looks bad, and a rebuild
> trade is a real trade.
>
> Collusion is two owners agreeing to help one team at the league's expense. Here it usually
> looks like one of these:
>
> - Moving a player for nothing so one team can shed salary or the other can absorb it.
> - Cutting a player with an understanding that a particular team will claim him.
> - Trading picks or cap space to settle something outside the league.
> - Trading anything that is not a player, a pick, or cap space.
>
> If you think a trade is collusion, tell the commissioner your reasons before the next kickoff.
> He rules within 48 hours, and reverses only when the evidence is clear.
>
> The commissioner checks every trade against the cap before it processes. A trade that puts
> either team over the cap does not process until that team makes a move that fits.

**That last paragraph should be adopted regardless** — canon does not currently state the
consequence when a trade leaves a team out of compliance.

## 7. What's Official

**Why:** see 0.1. Also: league votes live in Discord, rosters live in MFL, and nothing written
says which is authoritative when they disagree.

> ### What's Official
>
> MyFantasyLeague is the record. If the site and a Discord conversation disagree, the site is
> right.
>
> MyFantasyLeague does not apply NFL stat corrections on its own. The commissioner applies them
> through Tuesday night of the following week. After that, scores are final, even if the NFL
> changes a stat later.
>
> Playoff and consolation results are final once the next round starts.
>
> Cap and contract figures are official once the commissioner posts them at the start of the
> league year. If yours looks wrong, say so before the auction.
>
> Votes run through the bot in Discord. A vote that did not run through the bot did not happen.

**Commissioner decision needed:** the correction cutoff. Tuesday night of the following week is
a suggestion, not a standard — pick whatever matches how you already handle it.

## 8. When things break (fold into The League Year)

No reviewed league has this. It matters more for UPS than for most, because **per-week pro-rated
salary earning makes a shortened NFL season a live cap question** — most leagues can shrug at
this and UPS cannot.

> If an NFL game is canceled or moved out of the week, players in that game score zero. There is
> no makeup.
>
> If the NFL season is shortened, the league year still ends when the rules say. Contracts still
> burn a year, and salary still earns only for weeks played.
>
> If MyFantasyLeague is down at a lineup deadline, the last lineup the site recorded is your
> lineup.

## Explicitly not recommended

- **A separate bylaws document.** Every league reviewed that split the documents has a stale
  second document. Put governance in the rulebook.
- **League-vote trade vetoes.** Dynasty Fantasy League, FFPC and Cheat Sheet War Room all
  abandoned them.
- **Unanimity as the general amendment bar.** It hands any single owner a permanent veto.
- **A standalone sportsmanship or code-of-conduct topic.** Every league that tried wrote
  something unenforceable. Behavior rules belong attached to actual penalties, inside Playing to
  Win and Removing an Owner.
- **Week-long dispute discussion periods, or outside-expert tiebreaks.**

---

## Suggested order to put these to the league

1. **What's Official** (stat corrections) — operational, uncontroversial, fixes a live weekly risk.
2. **Changing the Rules** — everything after this is legitimised by it.
3. **Consolation reseeding** — one sentence, closes the tanking hole without touching the 1.01 design.
4. **The Commissioner** and **Settling Disputes** — write down what already happens.
5. **Collusion** — mostly codifies existing practice; the cap-compliance clause is the new part.
6. **Joining and Leaving** — least urgent until a team actually opens up, and worst to write in a hurry once one does.
