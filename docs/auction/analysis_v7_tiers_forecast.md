## 1. The Five Tiers

Every FA win in league history sorts into one of five price buckets. The single most important fact below: **the more a player costs, the earlier he clears.** Studs are decided in the opening quarter of the auction; the back third is almost entirely $1–2K scraps.

| Tier | $ Range | % of all wins (n=1,259) | When it clears (avg % through) | % in last third | Who plays there |
|------|---------|------------------------|-------------------------------|-----------------|-----------------|
| **T1 — Fodder** | $1K | 46.6% (587) | 51.4% | 33.9% | Everyone; the volume game |
| **T2 — Cap-free depth** | $2–4K | 32.2% (406) | 35.1% | 11.3% | Everyone; the dominant real-money tier |
| **T3 — Low-commit** | $5–9K | 10.6% (133) | 25.6% | 2.3% | Selective starters |
| **T4 — MID** | $10–17K | **4.9% (62)** | 19.8% | **1.6%** | Mannila 0011, Ryan 0001, Cutting 0004 — and almost no one else |
| **T5 — Marquee** | $18K+ | 5.6% (71) | 13.5% | **0.0%** | The 7 teams holding ≥$50K room |

Tiers 1 + 2 together are **78.8% of every win** — the auction is won on volume of cheap labor, not on trophies.

**T1 — Fodder ($1K).** Nearly half of all wins. This is roster-filler bid up by no one; it spreads latest (34% lands in the final third). Your natural zone — run it in volume in the back half.

**T2 — Cap-free depth ($2–4K).** The dominant real-money tier and where the league's bidding energy actually concentrates. $4K is the cap-free-cut ceiling and a *contested terminal price*, not a safe nominate-and-hold (see §3).

**T3 — Low-commit ($5–9K).** A thin transitional band; clears by ~26% through. Useful for a known starter you don't want to chase into the mid-tier.

**T4 — MID ($10–17K) — the league's blind spot and your biggest opening.** Only 5% of wins land here, and it clears at ~20% through with just 1.6% in the last third. Only Mannila (.103), Ryan (.083) and Cutting (.082) meaningfully contest it; everyone else — including you — sits under 7%. A disciplined mid bidder faces a thin field that clears early: thin + early = repeatable value. **In SF, the mid-tier is largely the QB tier** (QB winning-bid median = $15K — see §2), so this is where a startable QB actually lives. *Caveat on your own posture: you are mid-tier-light (SF mid zone-mix .027, below the league median) but not the extreme — four owners (0003 .015, 0007 .018, 0006 .021, 0012 .023) sit below you. The point isn't that you're uniquely absent; it's that the whole tier is under-contested and you've essentially never operated in it, so it's greenfield for you.*

**T5 — Marquee ($18K+).** One-shot and early: closes at 13.5% through with **zero** last-third action. There is no late discount on elite talent — you commit up front or you miss. (Read §2 carefully before pricing this tier: the historical sample is QB-poor and understates a real stud.)

## 2. Talent Forecast — What THIS Year Should Cost

**The SF-era marquee tier was paid for low-ADP QBs, not studs.** Across the 12 marquee QB wins, the median *and* mean positional ADP is **QB21** — streamer/QB2 arms bid up purely by SuperFlex scarcity. The top dollars went to the *cheapest* names by ADP:

|----|-------|---------------|-------------|
| Brady | $66K | QB14 | 109 |
| Herbert | $51K | QB20 | 149 |
| Rodgers | $50K | QB21 | 173 |
| Carr | $38K | QB21 | 183 |
| Cousins | $37K | QB22 | 192 |
| Tua | $29K | QB23 | 206 |
| Ryan | $28K | QB25 | 215 |
| *Mayfield* | $31K | QB7 | 58 |

Nine of twelve sat in the QB13–25 band. **No genuine top-12 SF QB has ever hit the UPS block**, so the historical $50–66K QB ceiling is a *scarcity* price, not a *talent* price. It was set when the best arm available was a fringe QB1 — the wrong anchor for a year with a deeper pool.

**The clean read on elite talent comes from the three true studs who hit** (positional ADP ≤ 6, top-15 overall):

| Player | Price | Pos / Overall ADP |
|--------|-------|-------------------|
| Tyreek Hill | $61K | WR2 / ovr3 |
| Austin Ekeler | $57K | RB3 / ovr4 |
| Christian McCaffrey | $56K | RB6 / ovr12 |

That clusters tightly at **$56–61K (mean ~$58K) regardless of position.** This is your true-stud comp — not the QB-scarcity ceiling.

**Pricing guidance for this year:**

- **Genuine top-5 positional player:** anchor at **$56–61K**.
- **A real top-3 SF QB (never seen in-sample):** budget **$60–70K** and accept you may have to top the in-sample ceiling once — QB scarcity plus elite talent has no historical comp, so the $58K skill-stud number is a floor, not a cap.
- **Fringe QB1/QB2 mirage (the historical norm):** clears $28–45K. **Do not chase a Cousins/Carr/Tua type past ~$38K** just because it's "the best QB available." That premium is exactly what burned past buyers.

The QB median ($15K) already sits at the floor of the mid-tier. *(Basis: QB n=40 winning bids — directionally firm, but it's the median winning bid, a reasonable-but-imperfect proxy for "price to land a startable QB.")*

**Budget posture.** Total league room is **$783K** (ceiling $300K/team) and rising as teams cut. **You hold $187K — the most in the league** (next: 0002 $131K, 0012 $118K, 0001 $109K, 0010 $92K); two contenders are underwater (0007 −$20K, 0006 −$25K and *must* cut). SF-era FA spend has run ~$670K–$1,048K/yr (avg ~$823K), and 2025 posted the highest $18K+ win-share of any SF year (8.8% vs 5.6% all-era) — the marquee tier is already expanding. **Seven of twelve teams hold ≥$50K** (0001/0002/0003/0005/0008/0010/0012), so at least seven rivals can individually fund a $56–61K stud today. Re-pull room on auction morning: if the ≥$50K contenders free up enough to bid $70K+, your $60K stud plan needs a cushion.

## 3. Timing Playbook

**Price sets the clock — and the gradient is steep and monotonic:**

| Tier | Avg % through when it clears | % in last third |
|------|------------------------------|-----------------|
| Marquee $18K+ | 13.5% | 0.0% |
| MID $10–17K | 19.8% | 1.6% |
| $5–9K | 25.6% | 2.3% |
| $2–4K | 35.1% | 11.3% |
| $1K fodder | 51.4% | 33.9% |

**Read:** by the time the auction is ~20% elapsed, essentially all $10K+ talent is gone. The endgame is pure $1–2K scraps. So: **studs are won early or not at all; depth is harvested late.**

**$1K nomination economics.** Fodder is 47% of all wins and spreads latest (34% in the final third) — this is your zone (88% of your SF wins are cap-free) and your high late-bid share (42% under 8h) is an *asset* here. Plan to sweep the back third for $1K roster-fillers after the marquee bidders have emptied their clips.

**Weaponize nomination timing.** Nominate players you *don't* want — or low-ADP QBs the room historically overpays for — **early**, while rivals' marquee money is still live (it commits in the first 13–20%). Drain their budgets on the QB14–25 types, then pick off your real RB/WR/$1K targets in the mid-to-late window after their cap is spent.

**The $4K cap-free tactic.** 117 wins landed at exactly $4K, but only 15% reached it as an opening/early proxy — the rest **laddered up** to $4K through contested bidding. You lead the league in $4K wins (15), and you do it the efficient way (only 3 of 15 were opening bids); Ryan (13) and Cutting (13) match your volume. **Almost never open at $4K** — that just hands the seller your ceiling and invites a $5K overbid. Open low and ladder up only on contested cap-free targets. Treat $4K as floor depth, not where titles are decided.

## 4. 0008 Bottom Line — Five Concrete Moves

Your profile: 88% cap-free, RB-concentrated ($171K career RB spend, ~3× your WR $53K; QB just $13K), 44 SF runner-up finishes, 2 marquee wins, 0 lurk wins, and you lose the head-to-head to **all 11 opponents** (worst 2-11 vs 0005). The gap is **conversion, not budget** — you have the ammunition and don't pull the trigger. Contender PF bar to clear: **$3,205** (range $3,084–$3,277).

1. **Attack the $10–17K mid-tier — it's under-contested and you've never used it.** Pre-commit 1–2 mid buys at $13–16K each. The tier clears by ~20% elapsed with 1.6% in the last third, so set hard ceilings *before* the auction opens and strike in the first fifth. Value will not drift to you here — it clears early.

2. **Use the mid-tier to finally buy a starting SF QB.** Your career QB spend is $13K — below the QB median winning bid ($15K), meaning you've been priced out of every startable QB. In SF, QB p50 $15K / p75 $18K *is* the mid-tier. One disciplined $15–18K QB buy converts your single biggest roster gap into a strength and is exactly the spend you've historically avoided.

3. **Re-anchor your marquee ceiling off the studs, not the QB mirage.** Ignore Brady $66K / Herbert $51K / Rodgers $50K — those were QB14–21 streamers. For a genuine top-5 player, pre-set a true-stud band of **$56–61K**; for a real top-3 QB, **$60–70K** and be willing to top the in-sample ceiling once. Marquees close at 13.5% through with zero last-third action — open at or near your max and go early, don't ladder slowly.

4. **Deploy your $187K room advantage in the first ~25% of the board.** You have the most cap room in the league and two contenders (0006, 0007) are negative and must cut. The early auction is when mid+marquee talent clears *and* when cash-poor rivals can't match you. Front-load aggression; your room is only a weapon if you spend it before rivals free up more.

5. **Keep the cap-free volume game — but as the back-half harvest, not the strategy.** Run your usual $1K/$4K volume in the final third (where 34% of $1K wins land and your late-bid strength helps). Almost never open at $4K. Treat the first fifth as your value window and the endgame as roster-filling. Pick a small number of must-win targets, set hard maxes, and top a rival's ladder by one bid rather than fading into another runner-up — that's the only fix for an 0-for-11 head-to-head record.
