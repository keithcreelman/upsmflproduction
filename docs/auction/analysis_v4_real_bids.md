# UPS FA Auction — Real-Bid Analysis (v4)

**Generated:** 2026-05-20
**Filter:** Strict July+, $1K wins removed, ≥2 bids required. n=663 contested lots over 7 years.

Replaces v3. Two big corrections from Keith's feedback:

1. **$1K wins out, period.** They're a different product — players going home for the minimum, often self-nominated, no real auction. Including them polluted everything.
2. **Reframed narrative.** Owners aren't "exploiting loopholes" — they know who they want, target them efficiently, and most of the league is happy to let those claims go through. That's healthy auction behavior, not gaming.
3. **New focus: sub-$4K distribution.** That's where the actual signal lives.

### What's shifting

| | 2019 | 2025 | Δ |
|---|---|---|---|
| $1K share | 34% | 52% | +18pp |
| $2-$4K share | 36% | 28% | −8pp |
| $5-$10K share | 18% | 9% | −9pp |
| $11-$25K share | 9% | 7% | −2pp |
| $26K+ share | 3% | 4% | flat |

**The story isn't "auctions are dying." It's a redistribution of energy.** The $5-$25K middle has been shrinking; both the $1K bottom and the $26K+ top are holding (or in $1K's case, growing). The market is bifurcating into "routine claims at $1-4K" and "real bets on marquee guys."

### Sub-$4K share (1K + 2-4K combined)

| Year | Sub-$4K share of ALL FA wins |
|---|---|
| 2019 | 70% |

| 2021 | 81% |
| 2022 | 74% |

| 2024 | 84% |

By 2023-25, **80-85% of all FA auctions resolve at $4K or below.** Only 1 in 5 lots produces a winning bid above $4K. That's the actual rare event.

### Reframe (per Keith): owners know who they want

This isn't loophole-exploitation. The pattern is:
- Each franchise has ~10-15 specific players they actually want to take a real shot at
- Everyone else is "I'll throw $1K at a depth piece and see if I get him"
- 80% of the league agrees on which players are worth a fight — those concentrate at $5K+
- The other ~150 nominations per year are quiet claims

A healthy auction. Not a broken one.
