UPS Dynasty · 2010–2025 · 16 completed seasons

Owners as NFL Franchises

Sixteen years of receipts. Every owner graded on hardware, team quality, how bad their bad years got, and how they actually behave — then matched to the NFL franchise they really are.

How this was graded
By owner, never by franchise ID. Guys have swapped teams and teams have changed hands. The 2010 title belongs to Tom Roussin at fid 0008 — the ID Keith holds today. fid 0003 has two titles from two different men. fid 0006 has two from Steve Bousquet. Nobody inherits a stranger's trophies.
All-Play % is the quality yardstick — your score against every team every week, so schedule luck is stripped out. Toilet Bowl: finishing 7th means you won it (mildly redeeming, still missed the playoffs); finishing 12th means you stopped competing.
Departed owners are excluded — but their seasons are firewalled out of current owners' records. Only completed seasons count; 2026 is still in progress.

League superlatives

Most cursed

Chris Klingenberg. Four last-place finishes, twelve toilet bowls in fifteen years, the worst All-Play on record, and a consolation bracket named after him. He also has the 3rd-best trade value per deal in the league. He's not bad at fantasy football — he's barely playing it.

Biggest quality/hardware gap — both directions

Blake and Whitman. Blake has the most titles in league history off the 6th-best team quality. Whitman has the 2nd-best quality, four #1 seeds, and one ring. One converts nothing into everything; the other converts everything into nothing. Same league, fourteen years.

Best process, no results

Eric Mannila. Best rookie-draft conversion in the league, 2nd-highest auction spend, cleanest cap sheet — and one playoff berth in sixteen seasons. Nobody has ever acquired talent this well and done this little with it.

The unpaid debt

Josh Martel's 2018. A 155–21 all-play record — .8807, the best single season by any current owner in sixteen years — and he lost the final. Winning the next two rings is the only reason this isn't the league's defining tragedy.

Most improved

Brian Cutting. Twelve years with a ceiling of 3rd place, then a runner-up and a wire-to-wire title as the #1 seed. His auction win rate climbed from 40.7% to 65.8% across eras, the largest improvement by anyone — after spending most of that time openly narrating his own tank.

Most expensive nothing

Keith Creelman. The highest auction spend of any veteran, the $94,000 Derrick Henry, 234 trades, the most nominations and waiver claims in the league — and a trophy case with two second-place banners in it. Sixteen seasons. Zero titles. He built the website everyone uses to look this up.
